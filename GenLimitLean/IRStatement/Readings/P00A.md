## Semantic characterization

Let α be a type. Assume that α is *nonempty*. Assume that α is *countable*. Let C be a *language family* over α.

Then C is *semantically inferrable* if and only if C is *condition two*.

## Theorem 1

Let F be an *effective indexed family*.

Then F is *effective inferrable* if and only if F is *condition one*.

## Theorem 4

There is an *effective indexed family* F with the following properties:
- F is *effective inferrable*.
- There is no *inference machine* M such that M *identifies* F and M is *conservative* on F.

[Lean declaration](../../GenLimit/Paper00A_PositiveDataInference/Results/Overview.lean#L45) · `GenLimit.Angluin.Results.theorem_4`

## Theorem 5

Let F be an *effective indexed family*.

Suppose that *language*(F) is *condition three*. Suppose that F is *condition five*.

Then there is an *inference machine* M such that M *identifies* F and M is *consistent* on F and M is *responsive* on F and M is *conservative* on F.

[Lean declaration](../../GenLimit/Paper00A_PositiveDataInference/Results/Overview.lean#L49) · `GenLimit.Angluin.Results.theorem_5`
