# P24 · On the Price of Privacy for Language Identification and Generation

Xiaoyu Li, Andi Han, Jiaojiao Jiang, and Junbin Gao study randomized algorithms on finite IID samples under replacement of one observation. The inventory follows the six principal theorems in Sections 3–5 of the 16 November 2026 revision. [arXiv:2604.07238v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.07238v2)

For 3.4, the public window has length `w = f(n)`. Its indices run from zero to `w − 1` in Lean. The required gap is stated for each earlier index, which expands the minimum in the paper and includes a first-index target. The signed empirical score has sensitivity `2/n`. Simultaneous concentration gives a score gap of at least `1/w`. Lean proves replacement privacy for the selected index, so releasing its language also preserves privacy. The Gaussian proof integrates the product density at the paper's variance. For 4.4, the score counts occurrences up to the public witness bound. For 4.5, it takes the supremum over all levels of the count capped at `gℓ/(ℓ+1)`. The unknown witness level and the distribution's mass floor occur only in the guarantee. The proof covers every safe language in the candidate window, including the paper's first one. A multiplicative Chernoff bound supplies the linear mass dependence. Sampling uniformly from the first `2^n` words of the chosen language preserves privacy and gives the stated `n/2^n` collision term. [The explicit theorem statements](../GenLimit/Paper24_PriceOfPrivacy/Results/Overview.lean) use P20’s ordered IID sample and randomized risk model. For 5.2, privacy is imposed on the released language even when several indices represent it. For 5.4, generation error includes both words outside the support and words already observed. The two distributions are chosen before the budget, algorithm, and sample size. An infinite budget imposes no privacy condition.

The shared proof tracks each coordinate of a coupled sample. A shared word costs no privacy loss and a replacement retains a factor of exp(−ε). Independence gives the product across sample coordinates, including the infinite-budget case. Mixing a common word of mass 3/4 with two different tails gives the stated base and both displayed lower bounds. For 4.7, choose a finite head carrying nearly all the original probability, preserve every word in that head, and redirect the infinite remaining tail bijectively onto the other language. The resulting distribution satisfies Assumption 4.1 and has finite overlap with the original support. Consistency forces high probability of eventually sampling an output outside the original support. A finite-sample hybrid argument transfers the probability bound back to the original distribution and controls the additive privacy slack separately at each sample size.
