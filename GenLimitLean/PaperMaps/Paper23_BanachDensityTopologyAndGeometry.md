There is also a converse across dimensions. It suffices to check the finite game on permutations of each board. Ignoring dummy inputs and repeats preserves all earned replies. If there is no uniform logarithmic bound at tolerance q, put successively harder finite boards in parallel lattice slices one dimension higher. A width-one rectangle in the extra coordinate preserves both the original volume and the target count. A single growing filter, determined by the board sizes before play, admits the bad rectangles. An adversary uses an untouched slice at each stage, presents the whole board, and completes an exact injective enumeration. Eventual freshness prevents later repairs. The resulting known singleton has filtered density at most 1/2 − q against each valid generator on some presentation. Thus the assertion for all finite-rank families and the assertion for known singletons have the same truth value across all dimensions and tolerances. For the implication back to dimension d at tolerance q, the singleton guarantee is used in dimension d+1 at tolerance q/2. The equivalence leaves the singleton assertion open. Fixed pairings cannot solve the finite game. Place an even n × n grid at spacing two. Its rows and columns are traces of positive-width rectangles of volume 2n. ## Principal qualifications and omissions

The paper writes real-valued ratios; Lean uses `ℝ≥0∞` so the nested infima
are total. Actual nonzero finite-window ratios are finite and lie in
`[0,1]`, and assigning zero to the unused length-zero case does not affect
the outer asymptotic statements.

The source labels both the lower Banach density in Section 3.1 and the
perfect-tower notion in Section 3.3 as “Definition 3.1.” Lean disambiguates
them by declaration name and section-qualified documentation.

The absolute one-dimensional density layer, perfect-tower topology, finite
Cantor--Bendixson ranks, repaired Claim 4.11, finite-tree Claim 4.18,
adjacent-pair Claim 4.20, and nice-schedule characterization are included.
Claim 4.11 explicitly requires a proper/distinct convergent sequence; without
that omitted source hypothesis, the constant sequence is a counterexample.

The theorem-card inventory now records the principal generation theorems,
relative-density conclusions, and higher-dimensional results even when their
Lean coverage is zero. The filtered-rectangle generation bound remains open. The original discrepancy inequality has a counterexample and the corrected lattice counting convention gives a global coloring. The alternative finite-rank proof covers the one-dimensional generation theorem.

For Claim 4.24, the source infers k ≤ log₂ ℓ from 2ᵏ−k−1 ≤ ℓ. That implication alone fails, for instance at k=2 and ℓ=2. The stated bound follows by using the available interior candidate at the final leak. Within a fixed visibility phase the left-leak distances satisfy x₁ ≥ 1 and xᵢ₊₁ ≥ 2xᵢ+1. At the last leak the greedy choice has distance at most ℓ−1 because an interior candidate is available. Hence 2ᵏ−1 ≤ ℓ−1. Lean verifies this argument on the rank coordinates and the availability conditions of a phase. The independent proof of 4.5 uses fixed spatial bins and requires no decomposition into greedy phases. [Lean proof](../GenLimit/Paper23_BanachDensityTopologyAndGeometry/IntervalLeaks.lean) · [Source interval game](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.02385v2#S4.SS2.SSS6)

Theorem 4.8 is a restatement of the #07 accurate-selector result and is linked
through that paper's theorem card rather than duplicated as a #23-owned
claim. Proof-only claims and lemmas remain documented by the source and module
structure rather than receiving separate headline cards.
