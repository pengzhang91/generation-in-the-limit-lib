# P25 · Differentially Private Language Generation and Identification in the Limit

Anay Mehrotra, Grigoris Velegkas, Xifan Yu, and Felix Zhou study privacy for the entire sequence of outputs when a single input is replaced. The five main theorems below appear in Section 1.1. [arXiv:2604.08504v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08504v1)

**Appendix Theorem C.5 is proved for Algorithm 2.** At epoch s the algorithm has received 2^s observations. It selects the largest public window of at most s candidates whose distinct pairs overlap on at most half that many words. The exponential mechanism uses the negative number of inconsistent observations as its score. The inverse-square privacy split sums to ε over the full output stream. Once the target enters the window, each incorrect candidate misses at least half the sample. The resulting error probabilities are summable, so almost surely the released index stabilizes at the target. The proof covers every injective enumeration, including adversarial orders. [Algorithm and Lean proof](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/OnlineIdentification.lean) · [Source Algorithm 2 and Theorem C.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08504v1#A3.SS3)

For 1.1, the mechanism considers increasingly long finite prefixes of the family. Its public schedule uses the largest finite intersection size in each prefix. Once the target appears, the per-epoch error is summable. This gives the existential theorem under Definition 1, where a fresh output avoids all inputs observed so far. Outputs may recur before a new epoch begins. This construction uses classical access to the public family. Formalizing the Laplace-based Algorithm 1 with the paper’s oracle access remains separate. [Countable construction](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/CountableGeneration.lean)

For 1.2, set u = m − d. For u ≥ 33, the probability of any mistake from the m-th observation onward is at most 10 exp(−εu / (8k(⌊log₂ u⌋+2)²)). The same generator covers every target and every injective input stream in it. The dimension parameter includes −1. The finite-sample proof gives the exact Appendix C.1 bound of 5 exp(−ε(n−d)/(2k)) for a natural dimension bound d. Each continual release samples from a pool large enough to avoid even the inputs arriving before the end of its epoch. For the positive results, the shared privacy proof extends an event inequality from all finite transcripts to the complete output stream. It also proves pure privacy of countably many independent discrete releases when each finite sum of their privacy costs fits the budget. Instantiating the release laws and proving eventual correctness are separate steps in the algorithms' proofs. For the necessity direction of 1.6, almost-sure stabilization gives the correct language more than half the output probability at every sufficiently late time. Causality lets a deterministic learner recover that majority from the observed prefix. Choosing its least index handles repeated names. The library's IID characterization then gives Angluin's condition. [Stochastic necessity](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/StochasticNecessity.lean) · [IID characterization](../GenLimit/Bridges/Paper03ToPaper08IID.lean)

For sufficiency in 1.6, the proof uses an alternative to Algorithm 3. At epoch k, each of k+1 disjoint blocks supplies k+1 IID observations to a tell-tale learner. Every target has a finite witness set that suffices for a correct vote. The probability that any block misses a witness is summable across epochs. The exponential mechanism selects among the first k+1 indices, using one unit of sensitivity per vote. A single observation affects at most one epoch, so parallel composition gives the full privacy bound. Repeating the last completed release gives a causal learner. ## The private identification barrier

The Lean model assigns a joint probability law to all released indices. Equal finite input prefixes induce equal laws on the corresponding output prefixes. Word and hypothesis indices are natural numbers. Identification requires almost-sure stabilization at one correct index on every injective enumeration of each language. [Continual release and group privacy](../GenLimit/Support/DifferentialPrivacy.lean)

The proof constructs an enumeration of one target in stages. At each stage it extends a comparison prefix to a full enumeration of the other target. Finite edits preserve probability-one eventual correctness under pure privacy. Causality then fixes a late time with probability greater than one half of naming the comparison target. The final enumeration has arbitrarily late such times, contradicting almost-sure stabilization at its own target. [Paired prefixes](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/PairedTexts.lean) · [Diagonal proof](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/IdentificationBarrier.lean)

## The size quantifier in 1.3 and the sharper bound in C.3

1. The informal theorem quantifies over all collection sizes `k` and dimensions `d`. For `k=1`, the ambient universe and the unique language are infinite. Both possible subcollections therefore have infinite intersection, giving dimension −1 under Definition 2. In particular `k=1,d=1` is impossible. [Definition 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08504v1#S3.SS1) · [Lean check](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/LowerBoundChecks.lean)
2. After equation (6), the proof uses a shared prefix for several possible targets. The success guarantee gives probability at least two thirds for each target individually. In the paper’s own `k=6` family, take a prefix from column `{0,1,2}`. With equal probabilities output a fresh word from columns `{1,2,3}`, `{0,2,3}`, or `{0,1,3}`. Each of the three compatible targets contains exactly two of these outcomes. Their common intersection contains none. This invalidates the inference used to establish the displayed packing bound. The counterexample concerns that step on one prefix. The paragraph below addresses the one-shot claim following Theorem C.3. [Appendix C.2, proof of C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08504v1#A3.SS2) · [Finite counterexample](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/LowerBoundChecks.lean)

The one-shot claim following C.3 requires εn ≥ k log 2 − O(log k). Lean proves a counterexample when ε can grow with k. For k = 4h, **every** family with closure dimension at most zero has a private generator that succeeds at time n = 1 with probability at least two thirds on each target. Randomly omit one of four equal blocks of labels. With probability 31/32, release the truthful membership bits of the observed word for the remaining 3h labels. Otherwise release a uniformly random block index and bit pattern. Every possible signal then has probability at least 1/(128 · 2^(3h)), giving pure privacy at ε = 3h log 2 + log 128. A truthful signal that retains the target selects an infinite intersection inside it. Drawing from the first 32 words of this intersection incurs collision probability at most 1/32. Together the noise, omitted target, and collision have probability at most 1/32 + 1/4 + 1/32 < 1/3. This violates the claimed coefficient for all sufficiently large h and any fixed logarithmic allowance. [Finite-sample counterexample](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/MaskedRelease.lean) · [Source one-shot claim after C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08504v1#A3.SS2)

The same leading constant also fails in **continual release**, even for algorithms required to generate almost surely in the limit. Keep the initial private word until the next public release time. Sampling from a sufficiently large finite pool makes its chance of colliding with any input before that time at most 1/32. At later epochs apply the existing private finite-family generator. Its public prefix lengths make the epoch errors summable, and these releases spend at most one additional unit of privacy. The chance of any error at any time is at most 21/64, strictly below 1/3. Almost surely only finitely many epochs have errors. This works for every family admitting guaranteed deterministic nonprivate generation from the first observation: a nonempty finite intersection could be exhausted as a common prefix, forcing that generator to produce another element of the exhausted intersection. Thus no such family of size k = 4h witnesses C.3 for all positive budgets. The private budget in this counterexample is ε = 3h log 2 + log 128 + 1. The proof leaves the sharper coefficient at a fixed small ε undecided. [Continual counterexample](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/MaskedContinual.lean) · [Source Theorem C.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.08504v1#A3.SS2)

For every k ≥ 2 and d ≥ 0, the corrected Theorem 1.3 uses h = ⌊k/2⌋ bits. There are two languages per bit, with the universal language added for odd k. Each language contains d common words and all suffix words whose bit has the appropriate value. The intersection of the full family is exactly the d common words. For any other subcollection, one suffix word in its intersection supplies an entire infinite column. [Hard family](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/PackingFamily.lean)

Fix a column z after the d common inputs. Causality and extension to a complete enumeration force the output to agree with each bit of z with probability at least 2/3. If a decoded output w agrees in a coordinates, give it weight 2ᵃ. Jensen’s inequality bounds the expected weight below by 2^(2h/3). For each fixed w, the sum of these weights over all columns is 3ʰ. Comparing the output law for each column to one reference law by group privacy gives ε(m−d) ≥ ((5/3) ln 2 − ln 3)h. This proves the headline order bound with a positive universal constant, using only the individual bit guarantees. [Marginal packing inequality](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/MarginalPacking.lean) · [Continual-release reduction](../GenLimit/Paper25_PrivateGenerationAndIdentification/PrivateGenerationLower.lean)
