## Observation clocks: correction to Stage 2

On printed p.1/PDF4, S_t is the set from the first t adversary steps. Set source x_j=`stream (j−1)` for j≥1. Then S_t={stream 0,…,stream (t−1)}=`sample stream t`. The element `freshOutput C stream t` therefore uses exactly the history available for source output a_t and avoids every observation in it. Its t=0 value is an extra initial semantic value with no effect on an eventual guarantee.

Stage 2 instead compared a_t with `stream t`, which is source x_{t+1}. Its proposed adversary choosing `stream t=freshOutput ... t` makes the *next* observation equal the current output, something the source permits. It does not violate freshness against the first t inputs. The claims of same-round weakening and a missing timing wrapper are withdrawn. Stage 1's prefix reconstruction was correct; the error arose in Stage 2's source comparison.

The warm-up state machine uses another consistent convention: transition/output index t consumes `stream t` and purges through `sample stream (t+1)`. It corresponds to source output a_{t+1}. That fact cannot be used to impose this transition index convention on the independently defined finite-scope output. Observation counts, rather than identical-looking variable names, determine the correspondence.

## Partial topology: an actual source inconsistency, no adopted repair

The domain on p.27/PDF30 is the language class X, despite the introductory sentence's “on C′”; its basic opens are sets of languages and the paragraph ends by specifying a topology on X. Fix infinite C contained in some listed language. Under the non-strict inclusion convention used for partial enumeration, every L containing C has neighborhoods generated by

`B_F={L′∈X : F⊆L′ and L′∩C is infinite}`, for finite F⊆C,

whereas every L not containing C has singleton {L} open. The source explicitly includes the full case C=K elsewhere, so proper-subset-only enumeration is not an available silent repair.

All full-C languages have identical neighborhoods and form one Kolmogorov class. Every remaining language is isolated. Given such an L, choose c∈C\L: B_{ {c} } contains the full-C class and excludes L, while {L} separates in the other direction. These opens are saturated for the equivalence relation. Two remaining points separate by their singleton opens. Thus the quotient is T_1 and hence T_D for **every** C and X under this definition. This is an informal direct argument about the printed basis, not a pre-existing Lean theorem. A literal proper-subset reading does not restore the proposed characterization: at most the point L=C becomes an additional isolated point lying above the full-C class in specialization, whose singleton derived closure is still closed; the quotient remains T_D. No such alternative is adopted.

The nonidentifiability test uses the all-infinite countable family X={N}∪{N\{m}:m∈N}. Its partial learner goal contains full identification as the case C=K=N, even with arbitrary infinite hypotheses. Suppose a causal learner eventually outputs the target on every full presentation. At any finite prefix of N choose an unobserved m. Extend that prefix within N\{m} until the learner outputs N\{m}; this finite extension exists by its assumed success on that infinite language. Then append m and the least still-unseen natural. Repeating produces a complete N presentation with arbitrarily late hypotheses unequal to N. This contradicts success on N. The construction can avoid input repetition if required. All targets are infinite, the family is countable, and the argument allows arbitrary semantic hypotheses, so finite-intersection representation or ordinary computability cannot rescue it.

The right side of Theorem 4.12 nevertheless holds automatically for this family under the printed topology. Thus this is a contradiction between the claimed characterization and its defined right-hand condition, stronger than merely noting the later “some/every τ_C” intersection-topology ambiguity. The PaperMap/registry diagnosis is incomplete but appropriately leaves the result unformalized. Replacing the singleton criterion by a finite-versus-infinite trace criterion could reproduce the later claimed trace-inclusion specialization order; proving that amended characterization is a separate problem. No uniquely justified smallest repair or matching Lean τ_C definition is available. Theorems 4.12 and 4.13 remain substantive coverage gaps, not accepted repaired results.

## Pods and other source repairs

For Theorem 3.5, the valid finite arithmetic is visible without trusting the displayed intermediate lines. If the four classes satisfy C≤I_m+I_o+G+B, both I_m,I_o≤O, and sG,sB≤O+I_m+error, then sC≤(2s+4)O+2error. This is the exact structure of native `fixedPod_finite_counting`, and the ratio certificates correctly keep the same output set outside the quantifier over s. The source's extra division by s and error-sign inconsistencies do not alter this justified conditional endpoint. They do not produce the missing dynamic pods or certificate hypotheses.

The Lemma 3.6 proof uses a large member of the cumulative pod union to infer capture in a particular newly created pod. That inference needs a historical-capture invariant. A generic example of an old large pod is not yet a counterexample satisfying the actual Algorithm 1, disjoint fresh pods, completeness, two earlier full stages and eventual cutoff. Even a constant universal hypothesis forces successive least-unused pods to advance, defeating a naïve arbitrary-pod counterexample. This review therefore retains an unproved source inference and a definite native construction gap, **not** a proved false Lemma 3.6 or false existential density theorem.

Stage 2's literal “minimum of permanent cumulative union repeats” objection also needs contextual qualification: the surrounding proof explicitly says to select the smallest **unused** pod member. Reading emission over pending unused members is justified by that context. The per-line algorithm notation is incomplete, but that display omission is not an independent mathematical obstruction once “unused” is retained. The downward pod branch still needs its supplying aggressive language made explicit. These source normalizations do not fill the dynamic native gap.

Several other diagnoses admit precise limited repairs. Theorem 4.8's display fails at L′=L, whereas its immediate prose excludes only strict sublanguages. The repair `D⊆L′⊆L ⇒ L′=L` is both contextually justified and implemented by the actual native tell-tale definition. Corollary 4.10's possibly-partial wording must be restricted to exact full texts for the native equivalence; the two-marker common-tail counterexample verifies that the restriction changes the statement. Its class can still be identified by conjunctions, so it says nothing against finite-family partial identification.

Theorem 3.3's power-of-two rule literally keeps choosing the smallest previously learner-produced integer, potentially omitting every later learner output from the adversary's full enumeration. Selecting the least such integer **not already revealed by the adversary**, with a fallback when none exists, repairs that enumeration defect. Its complete sharpness/counting theorem remains unformalized. The general-α construction also needs a controlled limiting relative density, rather than only “at least α,” before multiplying upper-density bounds. Neither observation refutes existential sharpness.

The finite-deletion proof claims a homeomorphism of original spaces even when languages merge. Take common={1,2,…}, X={common, common∪{0}}, W={0}, and C=common. Both languages are infinite; deletion maps two original points to one, so cannot be a homeomorphism. Their indistinguishability quotients here are both one point, illustrating why a quotient-level repair is plausible but not proving general deletion invariance. No native partial-deletion theorem exists.

Finally, Example 6 really uses gcd of observed values, not translated differences. For target {2+3k} and infinite C={5,8,11,…}, its eventual gcd is 1 and the suggested language 5+N contains 6 outside the target. A justified informal repair uses the eventual minimum a of C and gcd of differences x−a. The minimum stabilizes; the nonzero gcd stabilizes after finitely many divisor decreases, is a multiple of the true progression step, and yields a+gN containing C inside the target. This is a repair of the named witness, not a newly Lean-checked example or a proof of general partial identification.
