## Eight target dossiers

### D1. `R.theorem_3_1` — facade line 53

Source: Definition 1 and Theorem 3.1, pp.3-5. The actual type conjoins infinitude of every fixed candidate and harmful language, representation of every difference from candidate 0, and `∀M ∃j`: the named `diagonalStream M` completely presents candidate 0 and harmful j, while M fails eventual correct denotation. The families and target precede M; j and its adverse stream may depend on M. This is stronger than merely choosing new families after each algorithm. No computability condition restricts M; the diagonal stream need not itself be computable.

The defining sets are not arbitrary replacements of the integer construction. Map `commonPoint n` to `2n`, `stagedPoint n` to `-(n+1)`, and `safePoint n` to `2n+1`. This bijection sends candidate 0 to all integers, candidate 1 to positive odds, candidate `(a+2)` to `Q_{-(a+1)}`, harmful 0 to negative integers union nonnegative evens, and harmful `(a+1)` to `Y_{-a}`. Thus the source's overlap, infinite harmful sets and safe tails are preserved exactly up to relabeling. The five-observation formal blocks are a complete adversarial schedule, not a claim of literal step-for-step identity with the printed phases.

`SafelyIdentifiesFrom` (`SafeIdentification.lean:94`) only requires eventual correct denotation. Generally this permits switching between duplicate names, unlike `IdentifiesInLimit`. Here the concrete candidate sets are pairwise distinct, so semantic correctness entails a fixed correct index. Native reindexing theorems at lines 126 and 145 address arbitrary hidden positions without a random law. Classification: faithful deterministic reformulation and strengthened impossibility; the source's informal random insertion is not a separate probabilistic component certified by this type. Every displayed safe difference is infinite; no finite/bottom branch is involved.

### D2. `R.theorem_5_1` — facade line 80

Source: Theorem 5.1 pp.6-8 and Algorithm 1 p.9. The actual skeleton is `∀α [Countable α] ∀G C, (∀i Infinite(C i)) → SGFamilies(G,pad C,pad C) → ∃M, SemanticallyIdentifies M C`. One M works for all positive presentations of every family entry. Its eventual representing index and convergence time may vary by stream. Duplicate entries are permitted, and stabilization concerns one correct index rather than the original presented index. The witness's type is `List α → Nat`, resolved by the explicit supplement.

`verticalPad` uses positive natural coordinates. The native equation at `Padding.lean:32` and infinitude equivalence at line 64 capture the source's crucial empty/nonempty distinction: even a singleton difference becomes infinite. G must handle independent target/harmful indices, including identical padded sets requiring eventual bottom. C, its semantic membership and chosen candidate presentations may enter M's construction; the unknown target index or future target data do not. `candidateHistory` at `IdentificationReduction.lean:152` derives simulated prefixes from the observed finite history, and the statement at line 510 certifies the named identifier at line 317 given candidate presentations. This is genuine native coverage beyond the existential facade. It also applies to nonempty finite original languages when such presentations are supplied; the facade itself assumes every original entry infinite.

Classification: faithful conditional semantic reduction relative to the proof's intended premise, materially different from the literal printed theorem. A solver only for an unrelated fixed pair of collections is insufficient; Lean specifically requires a solver for the padded arbitrary C. The source explicitly gives a candidate membership oracle and computable encoding; Lean asserts no oracle-relative computability or uniform executable map from G to M. The native construction is stronger in naming a causal witness, weaker in computational content, and specialized in its facade's infinite-family premise. These changes cannot be combined into one unconditional strength ordering.

### D3. `R.theorem_6_1` — facade line 98

Source: DIFF-EMPTY and Theorem 6.1, p.10. The target is `¬∃P, ComputablePred P ∧ ∀pair, Valid pair → (P pair ↔ EmptyDifference pair)`. Both coordinates are `Nat.Partrec.Code`. Each valid code must terminate on every natural input with a Boolean encoding, and each accepted set must be infinite. Acceptance is output exactly 1. The correctness promise is restricted to valid pairs, while P is a total computable predicate on all pairs; its invalid-input behavior is unrestricted. The validity promise is not alleged decidable.

The source's bare semantic-set language lacks an input coding. Its proof's decidable-membership construction supports this concrete reading, but does not uniquely prescribe it. `MachineEncoding.lean:179-197` separately states a computable hard-pair map, validity for every code and the exact emptiness equivalence. These native certificates make the represented theorem concrete; it is not merely D5 with an unprovided embedding hypothesis. No learner, convergence condition, oracle or probability distribution is involved.

Classification: faithful precise specialization of the source's representation-underspecified undecidability claim. Infinitude of both accepted languages does not remove empty or finite differences from the promise domain. The formal statement excludes total ordinary deciders agreeing on that domain. It does not separately state nonexistence of a partial procedure terminating only on valid inputs, or cover arbitrary alternative encodings. One must not infer those stronger representation claims from the facade alone. The explicit pair `Primcodable` application resolves a hidden typeclass; it adds no decidability promise.

### D4. `R.theorem_6_1_restricted` — facade line 106

Source relation: a supporting hard subfamily for Theorem 6.1, not a separately numbered source result. The type is `¬ComputablePred DiffEmptyInstance`, on every machine code. Expand `DiffEmpty.lean:35-48`: left is all naturals; right contains every even number and odd `2m+1` when evaluation of c on zero has not succeeded within fuel m. Thus the difference is the set of odd positions whose bounded evaluation succeeds. This differs from the source's exact-first-halting-step witness on p.10, whose difference is empty or singleton. The change does not change the emptiness/nonhalting reduction. Classification: faithful hard-instance specialization of the undecidability claim, with an alternate witness construction. ### D5. `R.theorem_6_1_of_hard_embedding` — facade line 111

Source relation: an additional abstract reduction lemma supporting p.10, rather than a literal source theorem. Given a `Primcodable Rep`, semantic maps `left,right : Rep → Set Nat`, and a total computable `encode : Code → Rep`, it assumes for every c the equivalence `left(encode c) minus right(encode c) = empty ↔ DiffEmptyInstance c`. It concludes noncomputability of emptiness on all representations. Witness dependencies are explicit: an embedding and its correctness certificate are inputs, not supplied output witnesses. Neither injectivity nor surjectivity is required.

No premise makes left/right computable or their sets infinite. This lemma is therefore not by itself a representation of the source's infinite decidable-language promise problem. The native promise variant at `DiffEmpty.lean:216` additionally assumes validity of every encoded instance and restricts a candidate decider's required correctness to valid representations; D3 supplies a concrete realization of that setting. Neither arbitrary semantic sets nor a pointwise decidability assertion can replace the required uniform computable map.

Classification: faithful generalization of the reduction principle, with hypotheses essential to transferring hardness. It is logically broader in representation type but conditional on supplied data, and its unrestricted interpreted sets differ from the source problem's infinite-set domain. Finite/empty/infinite differences are allowed outside what the embedding equivalence forces. There is no learner, information channel or convergence claim.

## Discrepancies and source questions for Stage 3

1. **Literal reduction quantifiers.** Theorem 5.1's printed fixed-collection premise cannot yield identification for every unrelated L: known disjoint infinite singleton families already have easy SG, while arbitrary L may be unidentifiable. The surrounding universal-SG premise and padded-C application repair that inference, and Lean follows those stronger premises. Stage 3 should state which theorem wording is intended; this is not an unnoticed additional Lean assumption.
2. **Corollary 6.1.** Finite-time decidability or a stopping announcement is different from eventual bottom with no known convergence time. The printed singleton-or-empty construction demonstrates undecidability of emptiness, but its SG requirement collapses in both cases. The diagnostic exposes that inference gap precisely. It does not refute the existential corollary; no matching native theorem was found.
3. **Remark 1 must be tested with the oracle's allowed queries.** Emptiness alone can suffice after identification if queries on residual sets are allowed: ask whether `(K minus S_t) minus H` is empty; K minus the finite observed set and H remain infinite. If empty, output bottom; otherwise find a fresh witness by candidate membership search. A finite safe difference is eventually exhausted in the complete observations; an infinite one never is. Thus no separate finiteness oracle is necessarily needed. If queries are restricted to the original candidate-index pairs, this residual-query argument is unavailable. The paper does not give that restriction, and no native theorem settles the effective protocol. This is an unresolved access-model clarification, not a demonstrated false remark.
4. **P* and the phase argument.** The boxed condition p.11 literally uses implications with containment antecedents; subsequent text needs actual candidates containing the finite samples. Those implications alone can be vacuous. It also needs complete progress of both labeled enumerations. The native theorem uses another family and does not certify these steps. A failed or incomplete printed diagonal argument does not refute Theorem 6.2.
5. **Global subsets versus safe finite-prefix output.** Appendix A p.18 and the proof on p.14 assert eventual selected-candidate inclusion in K. Finite-prefix KM criticality establishes the needed membership of the chosen output within the tested vocabulary; global inclusion does not follow. For example, let the first candidate be the evens K, and later candidate n be K plus a distinct very large odd point. Along increasing even observations, the newest candidate can remain finite-prefix critical while that odd point lies beyond the search cutoff; it is never a global subset of K. This challenges the explanatory assertion, not the KM generation theorem or necessarily Theorem 6.3. Lean's global inclusion test bypasses this issue with stronger semantic access.
6. **Semantic versus effective claims.** Only D3-D5 and native machine-code certificates explicitly quantify ordinary computation. D1/D7 rule out arbitrary causal functions, which strengthens their negative direction. D2/D8 and the semantic Angluin background do not certify effective reductions, telltale enumeration or algorithms. No uniform program or oracle-relative code can be inferred from classical choice, countability or pointwise set membership.
