## Mathematical checks of the disputed cases

**1. Claim 3.2's null/zero witness.** Source pp.7-8 permits no-candidate score zero and an empty stored collection, but then literally demands that collection remain an argmax candidate containing its target. Let U=Nat, L1 the evens and L2 the odds; complete the indexed family with duplicates if an infinite sequence is desired. Both languages and U are countably infinite, and duplicates are expressly allowed. There is no source restriction excluding disjoint languages, requiring nonidentifiability or imposing distributions here.

At stage 1 no finite-intersection subcollection containing L1 exists, so its stored witness is empty and score zero. When L2 is inserted, {L1,L2} has empty intersection and score zero. Equality triggers the swap. L2 reaches the first position; L1 moves to the second. In L1's new prefix, the pair is now feasible, so the argmax is nonempty, but the old empty collection still does not contain L1. Therefore literal stored-witness membership is false even beyond an initially empty argmax. The empty-maximum/null convention on p.5 cannot make an infeasible collection contain the target.

The smallest useful repair is an unconditional maximum-value bound, with actual witness attainment asserted only when available (in particular at positive score). Every later feasible intersection has size at most the stored complexity. Lean implements exactly that distinction through `MaxScoreBound`, `OrderMaxScoreBounds` and positive-score witness fields, retaining the numerical score and equality-swap rule. This is a justified, disclosed local source correction, not literal equivalence to Claim 3.2. The counterexample and repair reasoning here are informal; existing diagnostic theorem interfaces support them but were not freshly checked or proof-audited.

**2. Noisy Procedure 2 initialization.** At its first diagonal pair (0,1), a candidate subcollection must contain that pair and lie in its singleton prefix. It is therefore the singleton itself, whose intersection is the infinite L1. This contradicts the finite-intersection requirement regardless of T, even if T is empty. There is no feasible witness to maximize. Noise, sampling or future presentation choices cannot change this combinatorial fact.

The smallest repair explicitly returns empty T/collection and score zero when no candidate exists; otherwise choose a genuine maximal candidate. Procedure 1's existing convention motivates this extension, but does not literally produce a feasible candidate for Procedure 2. `totalNoisyStepSample`, `totalNoisyStepWitness` and their cardinality score implement it. `NoisyWitness:235` states the obstruction with the explicit infinite-language hypothesis. Concrete stage/witness construction and `NoisyProcedure:1378/:1394` support the corrected noisy certificate/exact endgame. These statements justify treating the target's certificate as constructed, while stopping short of certifying every proof. Disposition: justified totalization with literal pseudocode non-equivalence retained.

**3. Representative C.1 and alpha=0.** Definitions 5-6 on p.14 explicitly allow alpha∈[0,1]; the finite partition has no requirement that every group be infinite. Take U=Nat, every indexed language equal to U, K=2, A1={0}, A2=Nat\{0}, and alpha=0. Both groups are nonempty, disjoint and cover U; all target languages are infinite and countable. The source permits duplicate languages. No nonidentifiability, full-support distribution, or stochastic input assumption excludes this instance.

For every m≥1, T_m={0,...,m-1} is finite and contained in the singleton selected language. The common fresh region is U\T_m. Its intersection with A1 is empty, so A1 is scarce, with empirical mass 1/m>0=alpha. Thus T_m is a feasible Procedure-3 scarce witness of unbounded cardinality. This tests every positive m rather than extrapolating a finite experiment. Claim C.1's literal boundedness fails; its displayed Kp/alpha argument is inapplicable at zero.

The actual generation claim also fails for this instance. Use the complete, repetition-free enumeration 0,1,2,.... At every positive time m, exact zero-error group representation forces **any valid probability distribution** G_m to assign mass 1/m to A1. Since A1 contains only the already observed 0, the probability of a fresh target string is at most 1-1/m<1. No finite threshold can satisfy Definition 6's probability-one freshness. This is a contradiction among permitted distribution marginals and freshness, not a failure to pick a valid law or sampler. The empirical law itself is a valid finite-support probability law, but is not fresh. Full support is not required by v1; adding it would not rescue this contradiction.

A minimal local repair is to require alpha>0 for C.1 and the ensuing general construction/theorems; Definitions 5-6 can still define the zero case. This removes the displayed division problem and this counterexample, but is not a certification of all remaining representative proofs. Lean's abstract target has no alpha parameter and implements neither this repair nor a concrete representative theorem. Disposition: justified new source-boundary correction, alongside an unchanged substantive Lean gap. The registry's “no-known-issue” should be qualified accordingly in future metadata, without editing frozen evidence.

## Deduplication, pair encoding and computation

Stage 2 called profile equivalence plausible. The following gives the missing **informal semantic argument**, without claiming a new Lean theorem. Restrict to the source's countably infinite languages and positive threshold profiles. Any finite injective clean history extends to a complete target presentation. Hence a source-valid G restricted to those histories satisfies Lean achievement. Conversely, feed a Lean-valid G the first-occurrence deduplication of any source history. Its length is |S|, it is injective, and has exactly the same observed set; correctness and freshness transfer whenever |S| reaches the threshold. One fixed lifted generator works for every language, so existential profile sets coincide, as do their Pareto comparisons. No true-target information is introduced.

For noise, the source bounds **outside occurrences**, not merely distinct outsiders. In a deduplicated source prefix, distinct outsiders are at most the original outside occurrences, hence at most n. In the converse direction an eligible injective prefix has equal distinct/occurrence counts outside L; completing it with target-only strings adds no outside occurrences and covers all L. Thus the same argument works for positive profiles indexed by **all** language/noise pairs, with a single generator. It does not incorrectly treat repeated contaminants as costing one source occurrence, or give the generator the true n.

Explicitly, for source i≥1,n≥0 set q=n+i-1 and k=q(q+1)/2+i-1. This is the zero-based diagonal rank. Define F'(k)=L_i and noise'(k)=n. Each pair occurs once; repeated underlying languages remain labelled coordinates. Prefix length through (n,i) is k+1, so Lean's k<f(t) equals the source entrance condition. Theorem 6 then applies simultaneously to every n,i; Theorem 8's finite sublevels correspond exactly to all-pair finite sublevels. The represented insertion inequalities and candidate constraints match the repaired pair process; no witness proof-step identity is required. This is substantial mathematical adequacy of the generic kernel, while a native encoding, rank correspondence and profile-transport wrapper remain missing. PaperMap's statement that coordinates “are encoded” describes the intended instantiation, not an exposed formal encoding theorem.

Pointwise semantic choices do not establish uniform computation. No numerical rate beyond the displayed observation thresholds is certified.
