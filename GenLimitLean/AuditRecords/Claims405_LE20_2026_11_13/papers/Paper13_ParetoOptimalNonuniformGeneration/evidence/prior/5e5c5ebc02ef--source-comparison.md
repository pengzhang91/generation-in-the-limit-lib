## Independent inventory of the complete paper

This inventory was established before Stage 1 access. It includes claims beyond the six selected targets so that a missing wrapper is not mistaken for an absent native result.

1. **Setup and Definitions 1-3, pp. 2-4.** The collection is an ordered countable sequence, possibly with duplicate languages; each language and the common universe are countably infinite. A presentation lists only target strings, lists every target string eventually, and may repeat. At chronological time t the generator receives the whole finite ordered history, without feedback or target identity, and must output outside the distinct observed set S_t. Limit generation permits presentation-dependent onset. Nonuniform generation has one threshold per language and works at every time with sufficiently many distinct observations, for every complete presentation. Pareto optimality compares every generating algorithm: improvement at some coordinate forces worsening at another. The p. 2 footnote expressly allows an unattainable Pareto benchmark; attainability is a separate assertion.

2. **Prior guarantees, pp. 4-5.** Closure dimension maximizes finite intersections over arbitrary subcollections. Prefix closure dimensions d_i and target-containing prefix complexities m(L_i) differ. Empty maxima are zero and empty argmax is null (footnote 4). The Li et al. bound is d_i+1 when the prefix dimensions diverge, otherwise max(i,c+1); the Charikar-Pabbaraju bound is max(i,m(L_i)+1). The first coordinate can have time one, precluding simultaneous coordinatewise minimization generally. Related-work discussion on pp. 5-6 attributes broader results; these are context, not new P13 theorem targets.

3. **Example 2, p. 6.** A concrete integer/prime-power family has overlapping 100-point positive blocks and disjoint infinite negative parts. The stated prior bounds are dominated after moving L_2 behind L_3 and L_4. The comparison holds at every other coordinate while improving those two. The displayed repeated prime powers do not affect the sets.

4. **Procedure 1 and Observation 3, pp. 6-7.** Insert each original coordinate into the already ordered finite prefix. Recompute the largest finite intersection containing the moving language; use empty witness and score zero when no candidate exists. Stop only at the beginning or a strictly greater score, so equality causes a swap. Store complexity and witness permanently. The maintained order is nondecreasing in stored scores; original-index scores need not be monotone. Every nonempty witness has another, earlier original language, and its other languages have strictly smaller stored complexity.

5. **Claims 3.1-3.2, pp. 7-9.** Improving m*(L_i)+1 forces worsening at some original j<i, a stronger location restriction than ordinary Pareto optimality. Claim 3.2 literally says the stored witness remains an argmax candidate at every later stage, in the prefix ending at its moved position. Candidate feasibility as well as maximal cardinality is part of that literal assertion; the empty/null case needs separate scrutiny.

6. **Algorithm, Theorem 4 and Remarks 1-2, p. 9; Theorem 1, p. 3.** For any nondecreasing unbounded scheduler f, run f(t) insertion stages at chronological time t. Greedily admit sample-containing languages while retaining infinite common intersection; output a fresh point of that intersection, or the universe if none were selected. With g(i)=min{j:f(j)≥i}, the bound is exactly max(g(i),m*(L_i)+1). The f(t)=t bound is no worse than the cited prior guarantee. Fast growth matches the benchmark on any prescribed finite prefix; the resulting whole-family generator is Pareto optimal with improvement and worsening both restricted to that prefix.

7. **Theorem 5 and finite-family corollary, p. 10.** If every sublevel {j:m*(L_j)+1≤t} is finite, one generator attains the complete benchmark and is Pareto optimal. The proof discusses a maximum-based scheduler and an inverse-entry inequality. The finite-family corollary asserts exact Pareto generation for every finite collection; it is not a necessity characterization for countably infinite collections.

8. **Proposition 3.3 and its attainable-profile characterization, pp. 10-11.** For all integer co-singletons Z\{i}, positive finite profiles are attainable iff every upper level {i:t_i≥t} is infinite. One coordinate above one can be lowered to one without destroying this condition, so no attainable profile is Pareto optimal. This family is nonuniformly generatable but not uniformly generatable. All its finite-subcollection intersections are infinite, yielding zero Procedure 1 scores and failure of Theorem 5's sufficient condition.

9. **Proposition 3.4 and Appendix A, pp. 11, 17-18.** For the family {1,...,100}∪Z_{≥i}, i∈Z, positive profiles are attainable iff at each threshold t=1,...,100 the integer cutoffs of coordinates with t_i≤t are bounded above. Single-coordinate lowering preserves admissibility, so there is no Pareto profile. Nevertheless uniform time 101 suffices, closure dimension is 100, all finite-subcollection intersections are infinite, and all Procedure 1 scores vanish. Appendix A's proof text contains issues listed below; the statement is inventoried separately from that proof.

10. **Noisy setup and Definition 4, pp. 11-12.** A noisy presentation covers every target point and has at most n outside-target occurrences in the entire stream. The same generator must work for every language and every finite n; thresholds may depend on both. Freshness excludes all observed values, including contaminants. A finite set is a-contained when it has at most a distinct outside elements. This set criterion is used for the combinatorial construction, separately from the occurrence-based stream model.

11. **Procedure 2 and noisy algorithm, pp. 12-13.** Diagonal traversal orders labelled pairs (n,i), beginning (0,1),(1,1),(0,2),(2,1), etc. Maximize finite witness-set size subject to each selected pair's own almost-containment allowance and finite common intersection, with the moving pair included. Equality swaps. The greedy generator tests each pair's allowance and infinite intersection. The description does not explicitly supply a no-feasible-witness fallback.

12. **Theorem 6 and Appendix B, pp. 13, 18-21.** One noisy generator achieves max(g(n,i),m*_n(L_i)+1), where inverse entry uses the length of the diagonal prefix through (n,i). Claim B.1 bounds witness sizes by a finite-intersection maximum plus the finite prefix size times its largest noise allowance. Claim B.2 has an earlier-diagonal-pair worsening witness; B.3 asserts later argmax retention. Arbitrarily long finite diagonal prefixes can match the benchmark. Theorem 8 requires finite sublevels over all pairs and concludes exact noisy Pareto generation.

13. **Representative setup and Definitions 5-7, pp. 13-14.** A finite partition into K groups is fixed, and alpha∈[0,1]. Outputs are probability distributions on strings. Empirical mass uses the distinct observed set, not multiplicity. Group marginal error is at most alpha in infinity norm at every positive time; probability-one membership in L\S_t begins at a language-specific threshold. A group is scarce if its intersection with the common fresh region is empty. Scarcity holds when some scarce group's empirical mass exceeds alpha, or total scarce mass exceeds alpha(K-|B|).

14. **Procedure 3 and algorithm, pp. 14-15.** Maximize cardinality of a finite set contained in all selected languages and suffering group scarcity, with the moving language selected. Equality swaps. Greedily admit sample-containing languages when scarcity fails. If no language is selected output the empirical law. Otherwise choose one fresh shared string per non-scarce group, assign its empirical group mass, and evenly redistribute the scarce groups' total mass over those choices. The distribution has at most K-|B| support points.

15. **Theorem 7 and Appendix C, pp. 15, 21-24.** The representative bound is max(g(i),m*_alpha(L_i)+1), with representation at all positive times. C.1 asserts finite witness maxima; C.2 has earlier-index worsening; C.3 asserts persistent argmax. Theorem 9's finite-sublevel condition gives exact benchmark attainment and Pareto optimality in this section's representative model. Its final statement omits the adjective “representative,” but the surrounding construction and proof explicitly invoke Theorem 7. The alpha=0 boundary is a substantive source question, not silently excluded here.

Pages 1-3 provide the abstract, motivation and overview claims; page 16 is acknowledgements/references. No additional mathematical claim is hidden by those nontechnical pages.

## Shared comparison model and strength

Write `P = GenLimit.ParetoGeneration`; all short Lean module anchors below are under `stage2/source-snapshot/GenLimitLean/GenLimit/Paper13_ParetoOptimalNonuniformGeneration/`, and interface anchors refer to `stage2/lean-interface/`. The six full identifiers are `P.Results.` followed by their dossier names. The exported target types and explicit types agree.

`HistoryGenerator α` is `(t:Nat) → (Fin t → α) → α`; F is `Nat → Set α`. No target gives G the true coordinate. `AchievesTimeVector` (`WitnessLowerBound:32`) requires, for every i,t,xs, threshold_i≤t, injectivity of xs and pointwise membership in F_i, then both target membership and inequality to every observed value. `PositiveTimeVector` is independently required. `RealizableTimeVectors` (`:44`) means one existential G satisfies achievement at all coordinates and the vector is positive. Noise achievement (`NoisyWitness:520`) substitutes `card(sample\F_i)≤noise_i` for clean containment, retaining injectivity and both output conclusions.

These are not literally Definitions 2/4: repeated histories are excluded, chronological length equals distinct cardinality only under injectivity, and no presentation/completeness hypothesis appears. On countably infinite source languages every eligible finite history extends to a complete presentation, so omission of completeness does not add an information oracle. For semantic existence, an injective-history generator can be lifted by feeding a chosen enumeration of each finite distinct sample. Conversely a source generator restricts to extendible injective prefixes. This gives a plausible precise equivalence of attainable profiles after transport. It does not establish that the same arbitrary G handles repetitions. `Support.Presentations` supplies completion ingredients; no supplied P13 declaration assembles this profile equivalence. The named schedulers factor through the sample set, making their repetition extension particularly direct. This is a missing bridge/assembly, not evidence of a false combinatorial result.

`ParetoOptimal A b` (`Order:27`) means `∀s∈A, (∃i,s_i<b_i) → ∃j,b_j<s_j`; it neither asserts b∈A nor requires competitors to improve coordinatewise everywhere. Prefix optimality (`GlobalInvariant:846`) bounds both witnesses by stage; the competitor remains a whole realizable vector. Earlier tradeoff (`Order:31`) requires j<i. An improvement and worsening cannot be at the same coordinate by strict inequalities, even without an explicit inequality of indices.

The type assumption `Infinite α` concerns only the universe. F_i may be finite or empty, and α need not be countable. This generalizes the source's universe/language domain, but finite languages may make above-threshold obligations vacuous. Empty and exhausted targets are not meaningful instances of the source's infinite-language game. Conversely the injectivity restriction weakens achievement obligations and enlarges the apparent competitor class, which strengthens its Pareto condition. These opposing changes should not be collapsed into a single “stronger theorem” label.

All constructions are semantic. They use arbitrary sets, classical maximizers and fresh choices, with tests of infinite intersection. Neither source nor Lean supplies a uniform computable coding/oracle-cost theorem. No effective algorithm, runtime bound, decidability conclusion or stochastic noise guarantee follows from the Lean types. “Time” here is sample complexity. The six targets have no probability law at all; probability-one and group-marginal requirements belong to the missing representative instantiation.

## Six target dossiers

### 1. `P.Results.theorem_1` — `Results/Overview:35`

**Source:** Theorem 1, p. 3; benchmark connection in Remark 2, p. 9. **Actual structure:** `∀ {α}[Infinite α] F stage, ∃G time, Achieves G F time ∧ Positive time ∧ MatchesPrefix stage time (c_stage+1) ∧ PrefixParetoOptimal stage (Realizable F) time`. Here `c_stage i=(canonicalProcedureStage F stage).complexity i`.

All four components are present. G and the entire time vector depend on F/stage before coordinate/history quantification. Achievement serves the whole family, with no formula promised on the suffix. For every whole realizable competitor, an improving i<stage implies a worsening j<stage; no suffix restriction is inserted. Positivity applies everywhere. Stage zero makes only prefix assertions vacuous. Lean zero-based i<stage corresponds to source one-based i≤n.

The benchmark is the actual finite insertion stage, not an arbitrary Pareto vector. `canonicalComplexity` (`ArbitraryScheduler:89`) selects the assigned score at stage i+1; stability (`:95`) identifies it with later stages on old coordinates. ProcedureStage's type (`GlobalInvariant:645`) includes permutation, sortedness, maximum-score bounds, positive-score candidate attainment and earlier/smaller-complexity witness members. `InsertionSplit` (`:189`) uses strict lower scores to its left and non-strict crossed bounds on its right, preserving equality swaps. Choice of a maximizing witness may differ from the paper's unspecified choice without changing the advertised properties.

Supporting `overview_theorem_1_semantic` (`GlobalInvariant:1537`) and `frozenPrefixGenerator`/`frozenTimeVector` (`:1292/:1382`) supply an alternative existential construction. A different witness is not a discrepancy: Theorem 1 does not demand proof-step identity or a particular named G. **Classification:** faithful specialization of the source game, with the extra exact-prefix component faithful to Remark 2; source-model interpretation needs the transport above. No lost Pareto or whole-family conjunct was found. Native result exists; this is not an absent-result case.

### 2. `P.Results.theorem_4` — `Results/Overview:49`

**Source:** Theorem 4, algorithm and Remark 1, p. 9. **Actual structure:** `∀ {α}[Infinite α] F f (hf:IsUnboundedScheduler f), Achieves (schedulerGenerator F f) F T ∧ Positive T`, with `T_i=max(entry_f(i),c_i+1)`.

`IsUnboundedScheduler` (`ArbitraryScheduler:38`) is monotonicity and `∀i,∃t,i<f(t)`. `schedulerEntryTime` (`:43`) is the least such t; the strict comparison implements membership in the first f(t) zero-based coordinates, equivalent to the paper's f(t)≥i for one-based i. Both terms of the exact maximum remain. Entry may be zero; positivity comes from c_i+1. Long plateaus and jumps in f are allowed. No finite-sublevel premise or Pareto conclusion is present or required by Theorem 4 itself.

The named generator (`ArbitraryScheduler:136`) forms the distinct sample S, scans `canonicalProcedureStage F (f |S|)`, admits sample-containing languages with infinite enlarged common intersection (`standardGreedyAccept`, `GlobalInvariant:1121`), and chooses a fresh shared point. Empty selected intersection means the whole infinite ambient type. The output does not receive hf; its bound does.

The paper runs f(t) stages at chronological time; Lean runs f(|S|). They agree on injective histories. On repeated histories these are different procedures, although the latter can still satisfy the advertised cardinality bound: |S|≥entry already places the target in scope. Thus a semantic alternative is faithful to the performance claim, but identification with the literal source algorithm on every permitted history is not established by this target. **Classification:** faithful specialization for the named guarantee; different repetition behavior, with a missing presentation bridge rather than an established bound error. Remark 1's prior-algorithm comparison is not separately represented; the native finite-intersection score machinery exists but the prior bound's comparison wrapper/derivation is absent.

## Discrepancies, source questions and Stage 1 corrections

The following source issues remain explicit questions for Stage 3, without adopting a repaired paper as the benchmark.

**Null witnesses and literal argmax.** Procedure 1 allows the stored collection to be empty when no finite-intersection candidate exists. Claim 3.2 still demands membership in an argmax whose feasible collections contain the target. Consider two disjoint infinite languages. The first has no finite-intersection candidate and stores empty; after the second is inserted at equality, their pair is a feasible zero-intersection candidate for the first. The old empty collection is still infeasible. Footnote 4's null convention does not make it a collection containing the target. Lean uses upper bounds sufficient for correctness and positive-score candidate attainment; these are not literally unconditional witness feasibility. `GlobalInvariant:1585/:1670` have statement types consistent with this obstruction, but the source counterexample above supplies the comparison reasoning independently. B.3 has the corresponding absent-witness concern.

**Procedure 2 totality.** At its first pair, a singleton infinite language has no finite-intersection subcollection, so the displayed maximization has no feasible T. B.1 bounds feasible witnesses; it does not produce one when none exists. Lean explicitly defaults both sample and witness to empty and score zero. This seems a natural extension, but it is a repair/totalization, not an equality to the literal incomplete procedure. Procedure 3 likewise lacks an explicit no-candidate/empty empirical-law convention.

**Representative alpha=0.** Definitions 5-6 allow zero, whereas C.1's proof uses Kp/alpha. More than a division notation issue is possible: take U=Nat, one language U and partition {0}, U\{0}. For every finite T containing 0 and arbitrarily many nonzero points, the first group is scarce and has positive empirical mass. At alpha=0, T is a feasible scarce witness of arbitrarily large size. Thus C.1's literal boundedness fails for this example, and no finite freshness threshold can coexist with exact empirical representation once 0 has been seen. Should the representative results require alpha>0 or a different class assumption? The audit does not answer by silently narrowing alpha.

**Appendix A proof text.** On p. 17 the implication “cutoff greater than output implies output outside the language” can fail while the fresh output remains in the common 100-point block; e.g. sample {1}, output 2, cutoff 3. The all-one argument on p. 18 also needs care because choosing cutoff equal to output leaves that output in the tail. These are source proof questions, not changes to Proposition 3.4's statement; the native profile characterization/no-Pareto statement may be faithful despite a different argument.
