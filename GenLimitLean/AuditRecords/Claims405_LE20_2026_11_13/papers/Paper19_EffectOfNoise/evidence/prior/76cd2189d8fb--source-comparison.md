## Common semantic comparison

For concise complete types, write `A_i(s,L)` for injectivity of `s`, `L subset range(s)`, and a finite set exactly equal to `range(s)\\L` of cardinality at most `i`. Write `P(g,L,s,t)` for output in `L` and outside `{s(0),...,s(t)}`. The native definitions give:

```text
U_i(C) = exists g, exists T, forall L in C, forall s,
           A_i(s,L) -> forall t>=T, P(g,L,s,t)
N_i(C) = exists g, forall L in C, exists T, forall s,
           A_i(s,L) -> forall t>=T, P(g,L,s,t)
UD(C)  = exists g, forall i, exists T, forall L in C, forall s,
           A_i(s,L) -> forall t>=T, P(g,L,s,t)
ND(C)  = exists g, forall i, forall L in C, exists T, forall s,
           A_i(s,L) -> forall t>=T, P(g,L,s,t)
```

`g` is `(n : Nat) -> (Fin n -> alpha) -> alpha`. The paper's finite ordered list and this indexed history are equivalent representations. Neither definition imposes effective computation. The function sees its ordered input history only: no target identifier, membership oracle, noise label or noise-bound argument. Class-dependent and fixed-level-dependent choices are semantic existence. One noise-dependent generator is chosen before every `i`; this is stronger than separately choosing a generator for each level.

Precise native anchors are `Core/GenericGeneration.lean:37,41,63`; `Core/FiniteContamination.lean:238,277,312`; `Core/EventualGeneration.lean:20,28`; P19 `Definitions.lean:25,42,55,60,67,99,106,112,120,127,132,139,144`. Here and below paths are relative to `stage2/source-snapshot/GenLimitLean/GenLimit/`; bare P19 files are under `Paper19_EffectOfNoise/`. The two support aliases are at `Support/FiniteContamination.lean:15,31`.

`observed(s,t)=sample(s,t+1)` and `outputAt(g,s,t)=g(t+1)(s restricted to Fin(t+1))`; therefore timing is inclusive exactly as in the source. Zero already sees one input. Freshness excludes noisy observed inputs too, but not earlier outputs. The underlying generic half-open clock must not be substituted without its `t+1` bridge (`Definitions:73`).

The native budget is on distinct values, not violation times. Injectivity makes those counts equal for P19 admissible streams (`Core/FiniteContamination:346`); repeated streams remain inadmissible. In contrast, imported P06 predicates allow repetitions, bound outside occurrences, need not cover the target, and use distinct-sample thresholds (`Paper06.../Definitions:94,101,118`). Their types are not identical to P19's. `Bridges:110,136` supplies explicit one-way implication to P19; imports alone cannot supply an unproved reverse implication.

For closure, `consistentLanguages`, `noisyCommonCore`, `noisyClosure` at `Closure:17,23,29` exactly realize Definitions 2.8-2.9, including the empty default. `W_i(C,d)` at `Closure:93` means a finite sample of cardinality `d`, a nonempty consistent family, and finite closure. `F_i(C)` at `Closure:101` is `exists B, forall d>B, not W_i(C,d)`. It captures finiteness of dimension without a numerical dimension object, maximum witness, or least bound. Empty closures qualify; inconsistent samples do not. The paper leaves the no-bad-sample convention implicit; Lean's boundedness predicate holds there, including for the empty class.

`E_i(C)` at `Nonuniform:31` means `exists layers : Nat -> LanguageClass alpha`, monotone by inclusion, exact union `C`, and `forall j, F_i(layers(j))`. Layers can be empty, repeated, uncountable, and individually have different bounds. There is no effective cover or bound uniform in the layer index.

No probability distributions, expected losses, support losses or statistical rates appear in these assertions. Correctness is deterministic membership plus input freshness at every time beyond the threshold. There is no last-noise-relative clock or stream-dependent threshold. Classical choice and arbitrary sets supply no ordinary executable algorithm or uniformly decidable implementation.

## Discrepancies, source questions and Stage 1 corrections

**Algorithm 1 differs beyond iteration compression.** In the source, the first test is literal set disjointness between a candidate block and previously forbidden output columns. Native `algorithmForbidden` (`Separation:577`) forbids every block index equal to the first coordinate of any prior output column. Internal block `j` contains only `(j,k)` with `k<j+1`. An output column `(j,k)` with `k>=j+1` therefore forbids block `j` natively even though it is not in that block. The other forbidden term, previously selected blocks hit by the new output, expresses the other source conflict condition; it does not remove this overapproximation.

A direct body-level example is a generator returning `((n,n+2),0)` on every history of length `n`. At block `i`, its output column is `(i+1,i+3)`, in no block, so all hit sets are empty and all indices are outside their own blocks. After accepting block 0, source Algorithm 1 would accept block 1: its block is disjoint from forbidden column `(1,3)`, and its own output is outside the selected block 0. Native `algorithmForbidden` contains index 1, so `algorithmRejectedScan` rejects it. This example needs no compilation or proof inspection. It establishes different acceptance behavior even after allowing reindexing and the alternative block representation.

`Separation:650` chooses an arbitrary allowable next index and counts accepted updates; `RejectedScan:29,97` instead enumerates outside indices in increasing order and counts all attempts. The latter repairs the iteration-count distinction, while keeping the stronger native rejection criterion. Neither has a supplied equality/refinement theorem to the literal source state transition. Classify exact transition semantics as not represented; classify the existing scan as a conservative construction variant with materially different intermediate states. Its separation conclusion is independently present as a native statement; this review does not audit why its proof works.

**Dimension and rates.** Numeric `NC_i` and its empty-witness convention are not defined natively. Witness transfer captures the substantive square-root lower bound, so calling Lemma 3.2 wholly absent would be wrong. The paper's Lemma 3.1 proof mentions explicit correctness after dimension-sized time and shifts between zero-based time and one-based prefix length. The supplied corresponding theorem types assert existence, without an explicit sharp threshold formula. That quantitative proof-discussion detail is not separately certified here.

**Source issues for Stage 3.** The paragraph after Algorithm 1 calls `C` a set of column indices and says `L` is their column union, while line 7 adds block indices `i`; literally `L` is the union of columns indexed by the selected blocks' elements. This is a source notation issue, not silently repaired evidence. Ask also how the source defines dimension when no bad sample exists, and how its Lemma 3.1 prefix/time reindexing is intended. None is automatically a Lean defect. The native stronger Algorithm 1 test needs an explicit developer explanation if literal algorithm correspondence is intended.

**Stage 1 assessment.** No substantive error was found in its four code-only reconstructions: stream injectivity, distinct-value contamination, timing, quantifier order, hidden separator domains and vacuous finite-target behavior are correctly reconstructed. Its statement that wrappers do not assert infinitude or name the separating witness remains correct. It must not be promoted into a library-wide absence claim: Stage 2's larger allowlist supplies `paperColumnUnionClass`, its infinitude and strong separation. Likewise Stage 1's fixed-level theorem cannot alone be read as a common generator across levels; the separately reconstructed noise-dependent components genuinely provide that stronger order. Stage 1 did not inventory the full source or Algorithm 1, so these additions correct scope extrapolations, not its exported-type reading.
