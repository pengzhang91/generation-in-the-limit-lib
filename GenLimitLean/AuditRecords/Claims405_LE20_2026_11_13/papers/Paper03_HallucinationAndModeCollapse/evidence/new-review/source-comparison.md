## C04 dossier — claim:kmv25:explicit-mop-undecidability

**Source.** Appendix A defines G1=G2=... as one input-free randomized machine: read ternary tape until 2, parse the preceding binary string as a machine/input, simulate it, and tag the halting output; otherwise output 0. Proposition A.1 asserts undecidability of support membership for this fixed machine. The interpretive proof intends arbitrary machine/input halting to be equivalent to membership of its tagged encoding. This is an explicit witness, not merely an abstract noncomputable support set. There is no target-language, training distribution or rate hypothesis.

DecidableMOP chooses a computable decider **after each observation list**; UniformDecidableMOP chooses one computable two-argument query before all lists. ## C16 dossier — claim:kmv25:subset-oracle-identification-without-telltales

**Source quantifiers.** Fix identifiable L and correct subset oracle S, then choose A independently of the target and its enumeration. For every K in L and every enumeration, A must eventually stabilize to one index denoting K. Membership access remains a standing resource. A receives no tell-tale, even though a finite tell-tale is used in the correctness argument. The raw algorithm need only eventually output an index of K; the source explicitly invokes its Lemma 5.4 to stabilize duplicate indices.

**Native declaration anchors.** H.subsetIdentificationProgram (SubsetIdentification:102; census 552), H.subsetIdentifier (:138; 555), H.subsetIdentifier_computable (:142; 556), H.subsetIdentifier_finiteWitness (SubsetIdentificationRates:48; 558), H.subsetIdentifier_last_critical (:25; 559). These seven-target-supplement C16 entries were independently reconstructed after the original zero-target Stage 1.

**Algorithm correspondence.** For xs of length n, query membership of every observed entry in each j<n and inclusion Cj subset Ck for k<j. A candidate is consistent if it contains xs and critical if included in every earlier consistent candidate. The finite postprocessor returns the greatest qualifying j<n, or 0 when none exists. This matches the source raw learner, with zero-based indexing. The program is a concrete TruthTableProgram with primitive-recursive finite query schedule and computable finite-table output. Both oracle structures include semantic correctness but no computability field. The ordinary Computable conclusion requires Computable2 for membership and for inclusion. Tell-tale sets and their enumeration are absent from the algorithm inputs.

**Finite-witness conclusion.** For all C,O,S, ConditionTwo C implies: for every i there exists finite T subset Ci, then N, such that every xs with N<=length xs, all entries in Ci, and T contained in xs satisfies C(A xs)=Ci. T,N precede xs and may depend on C,O,S,i. The type does not identify T with a particular source tell-tale or provide a program choosing it. This is stronger than eventual correctness along one enumeration, while its ConditionTwo hypothesis asks only for existence of finite tell-tales. It is not a rate or fixed-index theorem.

**Missing component and strength.** GenLimit.IdentifiesInLimit requires `exists name, denote name=target and exists cutoff, forall later times, guess=name`. The finite-witness conclusion gives only `C(A xs)=Ci`; duplicate indices can vary. In an all-equal infinite family, every candidate is critical and the raw last-critical output increases with n, illustrating the distinction without claiming a proof-body defect. The source anticipates this and specifies separate projection post-processing. It was not located as a native composition for this learner. The overall verdict is `partial`: faithful raw algorithm and extensional guarantee, with the source's fixed-name completion missing from checked native coverage. Opposing changes prevent calling the entire result simply stronger or weaker.

**Boundary cases.** Empty xs returns 0 and the last-critical theorem is vacuous without k<n. Repeated observations increase n and may repeat queries. Duplicate languages are allowed. Empty languages have no Nat-valued presentation under range equality; the finite-witness predicate may be vacuous for a positive threshold. No infinitude hypothesis is needed by the subset selector itself. These facts do not supply generation from exhausted targets.

## C14 dossier — claim:kmv25:subset-oracle-best-of-both-worlds-generation

**Source quantifiers and witness.** For each fixed countable L and correct subset oracle S, choose one G before the target and enumeration. G consistently generates unseen target strings for every target enumeration. If that same L is identifiable, that same G eventually has support exactly K minus the seen sample. It requires no tell-tale input. These are support-level and same-witness properties; two existential generators chosen under different premises would not suffice.

**Native anchors.** H.identifiedBreadth_support (BreadthConstruction:25; census 437) and H.identifiedBreadth_online (OnlineBreadthConstruction:19; 434), with direct definitions H.identifiedBreadth, H.exactFreshSampler, H.FreshBreadthInLimit and OracleFamily.mk. These are the supplement's two C14 endpoints.

For every packaged O, arbitrary learner A and list xs, the support theorem states exactly `support(identifiedBreadth O A xs) = O.language(A xs) minus xs`. O packages **infinitude of every indexed language** and a correct membership query. The law is the pushforward of a fixed geometric seed distribution through a named search for an unseen selected-language member at or above the seed. It is a full-support law, not a uniform distribution. Infinite selected languages ensure the fresh set is never exhausted.

The online theorem assumes A already **stably identifies** O.language and concludes FreshBreadthInLimit for this generator. Its quantifiers preserve one fixed A, all target indices and presentations, then a stream-specific cutoff and equality at all later times. It imposes no computability of A or O.query. The wrapped support Bool uses classical proposition decidability, so the Bool field alone does not establish an executable support oracle.

**Coverage and strength.** Exact fresh support conditional on the predicted index is faithful to the intended breadth conclusion and is more explicit about the sampler law. The online premise is stronger than the source's requirement that the family be identifiable plus its specified subset procedure: it assumes an already successful learner as input. It supplies neither a subset-oracle learner premise nor a universal non-identifiable-family consistency result. The C16 raw selector and this sampler suggest the same candidate composition, but the checked native statements do not jointly establish its two required properties. No full native same-witness corollary was found by the census search. Verdict: `partial`, with a faithful all-infinite-language support construction and a faithful identified-learner specialization, not complete representation of Corollary B.2. This is a coverage gap, not an assertion that the candidate composition is mathematically false.

No ordinary-computation or MOP claim is inferred from these two semantic signatures; separate native effectiveness locators were not used as substitutes for the missing same-witness guarantee. Arbitrary correct subset access can be noncomputable. Finite/exhausted-target variants are outside OracleFamily; whether Corollary B.2 is intended beyond the paper's usual infinite-language generation convention remains a source-scope question.
