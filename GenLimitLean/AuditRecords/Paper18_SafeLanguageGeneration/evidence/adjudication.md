## Mathematical adjudication of the repairs

### Fixed families, hidden positions and the identification reduction

For Theorem 3.1, the three spines are independently infinite. Map common n to even `2n`, staged n to `-(n+1)`, safe n to odd `2n+1`. The fixed universal target minus harmful `(a+1)` equals the represented safe tail `(a+2)`; its difference with harmful 0 is the safe spine at index 1. These families and target do not depend on the learner. The named append-only stream does depend on it. All relevant differences are infinite, and each harmful set has the entire common spine. Thus neither finite-set degeneracy nor an absent safe-name promise supplies the impossibility.

`SafeIdentification.lean:145` transports the learner by the inverse permutation along with the family. Stage 2's shorthand that this “addresses arbitrary hidden positions” was too broad: it establishes equivariance, not an unchanged-controller guarantee under a hidden random index. The fixed-family theorem quantifies every deterministic history identifier even with the family known, so no random placement is needed for that deterministic obstruction. It does not establish a randomized learner failure probability. The smallest repair is to state the deterministic fixed-family theorem and separately leave probabilistic semantics unspecified; Lean does so.

For Theorem 5.1, padding preserves emptiness and makes every nonempty difference infinite. Candidate i is the simulated target; the unknown original target is the simulated harmful language. A safe solver returns bottom eventually on equal languages and nonbottom on strict supersets. Earlier candidates not containing the target are eventually inconsistent; finitely many earlier indices yield one threshold. Taking the least consistent bottom-returning index therefore handles duplicates by the first equivalent name.

The actual data-flow check is stronger than a declaration name: `verticalizedStream_source_le` (`IdentificationReduction.lean:55`) bounds every referenced source position; `candidateHistory_eq_run_prefix` (line 165) identifies the replayed history with exactly `2t` occurrences of one fixed run. `candidateRun_safe_difference_dichotomy` (line 190) includes presentation validity and both difference cases. The named identifier at line 317 has a family-wide correctness interface at line 510 given fixed candidate presentations. No future observation or actual target index is an input. Replaying finite histories is legitimate when they are such prefixes; rebuilding finite inputs is not intrinsically invalid. The source pseudocode leaves its ordering/run interpretation unspecified, which is the defensible correction claim.

A separate printed-quantifier defect survives that repair. Easy SG on fixed disjoint infinite families, for example evens versus odds with the least unseen even output, cannot identify every unrelated family. The family of evens and all one-point deletions is already semantically nonidentifiable. The minimal theorem premise must require SG on the padded arbitrary family being identified, or a universal solver covering it. Lean uses precisely that conditional premise. It also requires infinite original members at the facade, while native line 510 accepts any family with the supplied padded presentations. The proof route is supported semantically; a computable vocabulary enumeration, uniform candidate membership access and an effective subroutine protocol remain additional implementation obligations.

### Concrete coding, and what the corollary diagnostic establishes

The encoding explanation is not supported merely by the facade's theorem names. `MachineEncoding.lean:56` explicitly gives, for every computable predicate p, some total Boolean code whose accepted set is p. This is representation adequacy, not a claimed uniform compiler taking arbitrary semantic p. The hard subclass has the stronger uniform compilation needed for reduction: `universalRightDecision` at line 83 is a total characteristic expression; one fixed universal code is chosen at line 114; `rightDeciderCode c = Code.curry universalRightCode (encode c)` at line 127 has a `Primrec` certificate at line 130. `hardPairCode` at line 179 pairs it with constant-1 code, and lines 185,188,197 state computability, promised validity and exact emptiness equivalence for every c.

The left language is all naturals. The hard right language always contains every even number; its odd complement records successful bounded evaluation. Thus both languages are infinite for every parameter, membership is uniformly decidable and emptiness is exactly nonhalting. A total promised-correct predicate composed with the computable valid embedding would decide nonhalting, a contradiction. This checks all relevant representation and validity premises. Choosing one fixed universal-code constant does not remove the stated ordinary computability in c; no uniform compiler from arbitrary semantic p is inferred. The main target quantifies total predicates on raw pairs, arbitrary on invalid inputs; partial procedures terminating only on valid inputs are not separately stated by that target. The transport target likewise requires its computable map and exact equivalence; its maps alone do not promise infinite decidable languages. These are qualified, concrete results, not missing embeddings.

The printed pair is different. `FirstBoundedHalt` and its complement (`CorollarySixOneDiagnostic.lean:24,30`) yield at most one missing natural. Uniform primitive-recursive membership is stated at lines 35 and 76, right-language infinitude at 110, finiteness of the difference at 101 and undecidability of this very emptiness predicate at 152. The diagnostic therefore does not evade the source's decidable/infinite-input promises.

However, Definition 2 requires eventual bottom for both empty and singleton differences. Constant bottom satisfies both; no required observation separates the cases. The diagnostic does not assert that every generator's early behavior is identical, only that success imposes the same eventual obligation. Nor does any fixed convergence guarantee supply an observable stopping time. Changing the singleton to an infinite set would provide eventual mode separation, but that alone gives a limit signal, not a finite decider: simulating a program and changing mode when it halts already separates halting from divergence in the limit. No identifiable counterexample collections or stronger decision reduction are established by the printed route. The smallest defensible repair is to withdraw that inference and retain the corollary as unproved pending a different argument. The existing documentation and Lean do this. No refutation of the existential corollary follows.

### Oracle impossibility and residual-query sufficiency

The alternate Theorem 6.2 witness has base family `C0 = E` and `C(m+1) = E minus {2m}`. Every member is infinite. Any finite telltale for E misses some even number, so that one-point deletion contains the telltale while properly below E. The semantic Angluin necessity interface therefore rules out identification. Padding preserves this obstruction through the repaired reduction and makes all its nonempty differences infinite; equal or subset pairs have empty difference. The witness families precede the learner and oracle, and there are valid presentations of these countable infinite sets. This tests the alternative route without relying on P*.

An arbitrary extensional oracle algorithm A specialized at fixed O is still a finite-history function. Since every such function is excluded on this family, even a constant A at a hypothetically successful generator is impossible. The oracle can decide relations between all semantic sets, including more than the source's infinite-set inputs; it does not reveal the actual hidden pair indices. Thus the negative statement covers effective finite-query programs as a subclass, although it does not define their syntax. The source's P* implications, missing witness construction and asserted observability of future non-occurrence remain proof questions; failure of that proof is not refutation of its theorem.

Remark 1 (p.13) is a separate sufficiency claim, absent from the native SG theorem interfaces. With identifiers applied to the two filtered complete substreams (both grow without bound because both source languages are infinite), membership access and an emptiness oracle accepting residual-language descriptions, query `(K minus S_t) minus H`. The first input remains infinite after finite deletion and the second is infinite, satisfying the source's input promise. A nonempty answer permits terminating membership search for a fresh safe word; an empty answer gives bottom. After stabilization, an infinite difference always supplies a witness, while a finite difference is eventually wholly observed and hence yields bottom. Wrong earlier hypotheses do not affect eventual success.

This requires a way to pass candidate-minus-finite-set descriptions to the oracle. The semantic Lean type permits those set arguments. A candidate-index-only protocol would not automatically permit them, and no supplied effective query language/compiler settles that issue. Consequently finite differences do not by themselves refute Remark 1 or require a finiteness oracle. The minimal follow-up is to specify residual-query closure and formalize this sufficiency separately.

### The positive theorem and its effective proof obligation

The current native construction uses full inclusion in `CriticalAt` (`InfiniteDifference.lean:54`) and `Classical.choose` through `Support/Fresh.lean:15`. Finite index scope does not make full-set containment decidable by finitely many membership queries. “Full semantic coverage” is defensible; the registry's unqualified “exact” label does not erase this computational gap.

The source's stronger assertion that selected KM candidates themselves eventually lie inside K is unwarranted by finite-prefix criticality. With K the evens and later candidate n equal to K plus a distinct sufficiently large odd point, a legal minimal-cutoff search along increasing even observations can keep selecting the newest candidate: its odd exception is outside the cutoff. Its output is still fresh and even. This refutes the global-inclusion explanatory inference, not the positive generation theorem.

An informal repair of the source's membership-query argument can be checked as three separate obligations. Assume an effective vocabulary enumeration and uniform candidate membership queries. At each round choose the least consistent in-scope harmful candidate j. For growing vocabulary cutoff m, choose the highest finite-prefix-critical target candidate n and search within that vocabulary for a point outside harmful j and all observations. Empty consistent lists in early rounds can use fixed candidate fallbacks.

* **Round termination:** with the round fixed, the candidate list and j are finite/fixed. Each finite-prefix containment failure, once witnessed, persists as m grows. Hence only finitely many criticality changes occur, and the chosen n stabilizes. All-cross infinitude makes `C_n minus H_j` infinite; removing finitely many observations leaves a point eventually inside the growing vocabulary. The search terminates, including fixed-candidate fallbacks.
* **Target correctness:** eventually true target index z is in scope and all earlier candidates not containing K have been eliminated by complete positive observations. Then z is finite-prefix critical for every m, so the selected n is at least z. Criticality at the actual output cutoff implies the chosen point belongs to K's prefix; global candidate inclusion is unnecessary.
* **Harmful avoidance:** eventually true harmful index h is in scope. The least consistent j is at most h. All finitely many such candidates failing to contain H are eventually eliminated by complete harmful observations, so `H ⊆ H_j`. Avoiding `H_j` then avoids H. Freshness is checked against both labels.

These arguments preserve all-pair/all-presentation quantifiers and allow stream-dependent thresholds. They support the source theorem under the stated effective access conventions; they are not a Lean implementation or an audited proof of the current native generator. The smallest formal follow-up is a distinct finite-query procedure and correctness/termination interface. Section 7's native finite-cross-intersection theorem (`InfiniteDifference.lean:301`) remains genuine family-wide semantic coverage; it does not by itself cover the weaker promise concerning only the actual pair. Naive-KM and conservative-strategy diagnoses and the complete executable Appendix A account remain outside native coverage.
