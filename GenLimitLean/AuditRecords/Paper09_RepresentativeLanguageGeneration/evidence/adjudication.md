## Mathematical tests of the disputed support claims

Let P mean the printed positive-intersection finite-support condition and E the native finite-exact-profile-points condition. The following are infinite arguments, not finite experiments.

**P does not imply E or either printed positive conclusion.** Set X=Nat, L=Nat, H={L}, A_i={m:i≤m}, and stream s(n)=n. X is nonempty and countable; H is singleton/countable and UUS because its sole support is infinite. Groups are distinct and cover X since A_0=X. Overlap is allowed in source Section 4. The stream's range is exactly L, so repetitions/presentation subtleties cannot remove the example.

For any subset I of group indices, the positive intersection with L is either an infinite tail when I is bounded (also L when I is empty), or empty when I is unbounded. Every finite intersection contribution is therefore zero, so the complete sum over all subsets is zero. This establishes P, including infinite index subsets; no distribution-support meaning is being substituted for finite support.

At every t>0, S_t={0,...,t−1}; its empirical probability on A_t is zero. Any distribution supported on L minus S_t is entirely in A_t, so its induced A_t probability is one. This uses nonnegative summable masses of total one and works for arbitrary infinite support. It does not assume a finite output support, full support, effective mass computation, or a sampler. Conversely, probability-one consistency implies that support inclusion for these laws, as exported in `Distribution:211`.

Thus every fresh-supported law has group error at least one. At a=1/2, feasibility fails at each positive t. For every proposed T choose t≥max(T,1); no eventual-feasibility threshold exists. A globally representative limit generator at a=1/2 would eventually be consistent on this presentation and encounter the same contradiction, refuting the full all-positive-alpha limit predicate. Native `TailCounterexample:93,97` gives zero finite-support size and P; `LimitFoundations:125` gives each-positive-time infeasibility; both selected counterexample wrappers contain all source premises. Exact membership vectors distinguish every natural number, so every exact cell is a singleton and the set of finite-profile points is all L. Hence E fails.

**E does not imply P, even with distinct covering groups.** Take two disjoint copies {l_n} and {r_n} of Nat, X their union, L={l_n}, H={L}. Let A_0=X and A_(n+1)={l_0} union {r_0,...,r_n}. This is a countable nonempty universe, singleton countable UUS class, and countable covering collection of pairwise distinct groups; no partition is required. Among target points, l_0 has a unique exact profile, while all l_n with n>0 share an infinite target cell. The finite-profile point set is exactly {l_0}, establishing E.

For every n, the positive intersection L∩A_(n+1)={l_0} contributes one. Infinitely many distinct singleton group-subcollections already force the printed sum to infinity. Thus P fails without repeated-group indexing or lack of cover. This converse example is a newly checked informal argument, not a preexisting Lean theorem claimed here.

PaperMap lines 140–146 correctly identify the defect and refutations. Lines 148–150 overstate the relationship by saying stronger. The smallest local formula change supporting the same-vector proof is to count **exact Boolean cells**, including negative coordinates, rather than positive intersections. The native E predicate implements finiteness of the points in finite exact cells. It gives a valid alternative theorem, not a logically minimal repair or necessary condition. Restricting to finite groups is another possible source specialization; adding P and E as a conjunction would be a genuine strengthening but would be narrower than the actual native replacement. No wording change can make the literal P implication true.

## The scalar group-closure definition needs a qualitative repair

The parent proposed H={Nat\{n}:n≥2}, with the single partition group Nat. Checking it independently: H is countable and nonempty; every member is infinite, so UUS holds. For any finite S, infinitely many n≥2 lie outside S, so the version space is nonempty. Its closure is exactly S union {0,1}. Points in that union belong to every consistent co-singleton; any other x is omitted by the consistent member Nat\{x}.

For a singleton S={x}, the closure still contains a fresh common point: 1 if x=0, and 0 otherwise. The sole group is live. No exhausted group has positive empirical mass, and condition two reads a·1<0, false for any a>0. Consequently no size-one bad witness exists.

For every finite S containing {0,1}, closure=S and its fresh part is empty. The sole group is exhausted and has empirical mass one; condition two is a·0<1, true for every a>0. Such S have arbitrarily large cardinality and still nonbottom closure. The same calculation works in the native Nat-indexed API by empty-padding the one-group partition: empty coordinates contribute zero and the live-index set is respectively {0} or empty.

Definition 3.1, pp. 5–6, consequently prescribes both GC_alpha=0 from absence at size one and GC_alpha=infinity from arbitrarily large witnesses. This is an internally inconsistent scalar definition. Forcing its zero branch would also corrupt the qualitative finite-GC characterization: after observing {0,1}, any fresh point x can be the omitted point of a consistent target, so no single fresh-supported law can work for all consistent targets after an arbitrary uniform trigger. The issue is not confined to choosing an optimal constant.

The minimal definition repair is: use zero when **no positive-size bad witness exists**, infinity when bad sizes are unbounded, and otherwise their largest size; equivalently take an extended-natural supremum with default zero. `HasFiniteGroupClosureDimension` (`GroupClosure:880`) directly says all bad sizes have a common finite bound; `HasInfiniteGroupClosureDimension` (`:887`) says they are unbounded. These are coherent and mutually exclusive, and implement the repaired qualitative reading. Stage 2 already raised the zero conflict, but its target classification and “numeric packaging” discussion were insufficiently sharp. Targets 1–2 now explicitly require this source-definition correction. PaperMap's “complete proposition-level encoding” description and registry cards 1–2 should disclose it. This candidate is newly checked informal mathematics, not a new Lean result.
