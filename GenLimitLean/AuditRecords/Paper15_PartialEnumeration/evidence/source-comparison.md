## Independent inventory of the entire source

The global model on p.1/PDF4 is a countable collection of infinite languages over countable strings, later identified with subsets of the natural numbers. The adversary chooses a listed true language K and fully enumerates K, or enumerates an arbitrary infinite C⊆K, one observation per round. An element learner eventually returns a string in K absent from the observations already received in that round. No requirement to identify K follows from generation. The initial success definition does not exclude repeating the learner's own outputs, although the constructive density algorithms do. A presentation must cover its chosen C completely; it need not cover all of K. The paper does not impose a clear global prohibition on input repetition; its sharpness construction recycles learner outputs. Neither input rates nor a computability/oracle model are formalized.

Definition 1.1, p.2/PDF5, defines upper and lower relative density by limsup and liminf of membership fractions in the first N elements of the denominator language in its underlying order. These are set densities of all-time output range O, not temporal output frequencies or probabilities. Questions 1.2 and 1.4 ask for the best universal constant and the possibility of generation under arbitrary infinite partial data.

The introductory headline results are: full lower density 1/2 (Theorem 1.3, p.3/PDF6); universal partial generation (1.5, p.4/PDF7); partial lower density α/2 for input lower density at least α>0 (1.6, p.5/PDF8); universal finite-conjunction generation (1.7, p.6/PDF9); and conjunction hypotheses containing C infinitely often (1.8, same page). The co-singleton example on p.5 explains why an individual listed-language hypothesis cannot replace finite intersections. Infinitely-often containment is weaker than eventual identification and gives no bound on the gaps between accurate stages.

Theorem 2.1, p.9–10/PDF12–13, restates partial element generation. Theorem 2.2, p.10/PDF13, promises eventually infinite hypotheses contained in K and infinitely many hypotheses containing C. Lemma 2.3, p.11–12/PDF14–15, claims translation between deterministic element and semi-index generation, together with equality of optimal worst-case densities. Theorem 2.4, p.12/PDF15, states the accurate-infinitely-often semi-index result. Algorithm 1, p.13/PDF16, orders the consistent languages, forms descending finite-prefix intersections, descends when the next intersection is infinite, and resets to a greatest common infinite prefix after a chain change. Finite consistent lists are padded by their last entry. Lemma 2.5, p.13–14/PDF16–17, supplies eventual validity, eventual comparability of consecutive identified sets, and infinitely many full stages. Its stabilization argument eliminates the finitely many predecessors of K which omit some eventual observation.

Section 3 first gives Theorem 3.1's α/3 lower-density guarantee, p.15/PDF18. The priority/token algorithm on p.16–17/PDF19–20 queues finitely many unused strings from aggressive guesses and emits least available strings. Lemma 3.2, p.17/PDF20, establishes eventual validity. Theorem 3.3, p.18–19/PDF21–22, asserts upper density α/2 and matching sharpness, using a least-unused adversary, occasional recycling, and a larger indistinguishable target for general α. The good/bad partition and Lemma 3.4, p.19–20/PDF22–23, provide a finite-exception injection from bad observed strings into earlier learner outputs. Theorem 3.5, p.21/PDF24, improves lower density to α/2. Its pod algorithm on p.22–23/PDF25–26 creates growing disjoint batches of unused strings and emits the least pending string. Lemma 3.6, p.23–24/PDF26–27, injects bad inputs into earlier pods. Equations (7)–(16), p.24–26/PDF27–29, combine pod counts, eventual disposition of pod elements, and a bound α/(2+4/s) at every fixed scale before passing to 1/2. This is one algorithm's output set across all scales.

Identification means eventual C⊆M_t⊆K, as explained on p.6–8 and in Definition 4.3, p.28/PDF31. It does not require the M_t or finite index representations to become constant when C is proper. Full data force M_t=K eventually. Positive results promise finite intersections; impossibility results allow arbitrary infinite hypotheses. Theorem 1.9/4.9, p.7/33, characterizes full identification by countability and T_D of the topology generated by finite-positive-data opens U_F. Theorem 4.8, p.33/PDF36, states Angluin's finite tell-tale condition in surrounding prose: no strict listed subset of L contains its tell-tale.

The earlier topology U_{L,F}={L′:F⊆L′⊆L}, equation (1), p.7/PDF10, is background from the density paper and must be distinguished from T_full and from τ_C. The new τ_C definition, p.27/PDF30, gives finite-positive-data/infinite-trace basic opens when C⊆L, but makes {L} open when C⊈L. Definition 4.1 and Claim 4.2, p.27–28/PDF30–31, connect true-language candidates, triangular approximating sequences, and nonisolated points. Claim 4.4, p.29–30/PDF32–33, uses approximants not containing all C as an obstruction. Remark 1 discusses finite intersections with C and illustrates why infinitely many trace elements matter. Lemmas 4.5–4.7, p.30–33/PDF33–36, concern the intersection of all τ_C, its limit points and Kolmogorov quotient, and versions constrained by a finite observed F. A signed-integer example illustrates different adversarial contexts.

The T_0, T_D, T_1 definitions and hierarchy occur on p.33–34/PDF36–37. Corollary 4.10, p.36/PDF39, characterizes separation by T_1 after wording that explicitly permits partial enumerations. Corollary 4.11 on the same page explicitly restricts to full enumeration and gives the inclusion-antichain criterion. Theorem 1.10/4.12, p.8/37, characterizes partial identification by T_D of each τ_C's Kolmogorov quotient; the proof promises a certificate-free learner despite C being unknown. Theorem 1.11/4.13, p.8/39, gives finite-deletion invariance after duplicate languages are identified.

Examples 1–6 are also part of coverage: prime multiples with conjunction hypotheses (p.29); co-singletons and a universal language, distinguishing −1/−2 tags, and full-model deletion fragility (p.40); co-singletons alone, finite classes, multiples identified by gcd, and an extension to arithmetic progressions (p.41). The conclusion p.41–42 restates universal partial generation, sharp α/2 density and the identification/topology promises, and asks about further adversary models. There are no appendices, experiments, distributional assumptions, runtime bounds, or sample-complexity theorems. References finish at printed p.43/PDF46.

## Shared semantic checks and component matrix

In Lean a family is `C : Nat → Set Nat`, with repetition of extensionally equal languages permitted. A stream presents E exactly when its range is E. Thus it covers every E member but may repeat and delay observations arbitrarily. All family members and E are infinite in targets 1–7; E⊆C z is the correct inclusion direction. One named learner depending on C and the stream serves all admissible E,z. E and z do not appear as hidden inputs to that learner. Finite nonempty families can be represented by repetitions; an empty family has no adversarial instance. Empty finite intersections denote all naturals, which supplies early-stage fallback and does not mean an empty output language.

`sample stream t` contains indices strictly less than t; `textPrefix` is their ordered list. This creates a real timing distinction for the finite-scope element targets: source round-t freshness includes the current input, whereas their `freshOutput ... t` excludes only the preceding prefix. A one-step delayed evaluation supplies the source observation convention, because `freshOutput ... (t+1)` excludes the current input and eventual target membership survives the shift. This is a small missing assembly/convention bridge, not missing mathematics for abstract generation existence. Taken literally at the same natural t, the target is weaker: for an all-N family an adversary can recursively choose its current input to equal this prefix-fresh output; the inputs are distinct because each avoids earlier inputs. Consequently current-input freshness is not an unspoken consequence of the target. The warm-up run separately inserts the current input before emitting and has the stronger native freshness statement.

`OrderedLanguage` supplies an injective enumeration onto its infinite carrier. Density uses its real liminf of rank-prefix fractions; the n=0 ratio is zero. An arbitrary injective enumeration need not be numerically increasing. Accounting results are valid in this abstract order; connecting numerically least-string run estimates to it requires an inherited-order hypothesis, explicitly present in the native charging fragment. No conversion to temporal frequency is justified. α is any real in the conditional accounting/endgame targets; positive α is only needed for the paper's nontrivial headline application.

## All thirteen target dossiers

### 1. `R.theorem_1_5` — partial generation

Overview:28; source Theorem 1.5 p.4/PDF7. The actual type quantifies C, stream, E,z, assumes every C i infinite, range stream=E, E infinite and E⊆C z, and concludes `GeneratesFromPartialEnumeration C stream z`. Its value is the specification `∃T, ∀t≥T, freshOutput C stream t∈C z ∧ freshOutput C stream t∉sample stream t` (`FiniteScope`:89,106; `Core/OnlineGeneration`:16).

`selectedScope` maximizes, among scopes 0 through t+1, those whose intersection of consistent original indices below the scope is infinite (`FiniteScope`:24,35,56,79). `freshOutput` classically chooses outside the finite sample. This is uniform in the unknown target and the presented subset, but uses infinitude tests and classical choice. Relative to the source it is a faithful realization of partial generation at the abstract-map level, with the timing bridge already described. It does not identify E or K, prohibit repetition of its own outputs, or assert density. Infinite E excludes finite-data exhaustion; empty-index intersection=N is legitimate during transient stages. No membership oracle or effective implementation is promised by the type.

### 2. `R.theorem_1_7` — finite conjunction generation

Overview:29; source p.6/PDF9. Under exactly the preceding four assumptions it concludes `SemiIndexGenerates C stream z`. Expanding `SemiIndex`:14,18,50 gives: each finite `selectedIndices C stream t` has infinite intersection, and some T makes that intersection a subset of C z for every t≥T. The selection depends only on the observed prefix, family and t. The two conclusion clauses are distinct; neither says the indices stabilize or that C is contained in each eventual intersection.

This faithfully realizes the source conjunction-generation claim, with stronger all-stage infinitude explicit. Finite index sets are actual representations; they are not arbitrary infinite intersections or a single guessed language. Picking a fresh string from their infinite intersection provides an element learner with the appropriate observation shift, but is not an extra conjunct here. Family duplicates and repeated stream values do not invalidate the semantic claim.

### 3. `R.theorem_1_8` — accurate cofinally often

Overview:30; source p.6/PDF9. The same assumptions yield two clauses: `SemiIndexTraceGenerates C (algorithmOneIndices C stream) z`, and `∀T, ∃t≥T, E⊆intersectionOf C (algorithmOneIndices C stream t)`. The trace definition independently requires all-stage infinitude and eventual target containment (`ElementSemiIndex`:48). Cofinality over naturals is the source infinitely-many-times assertion, including arbitrarily late times after the validity threshold.

The named recurrence starts at chosen index zero, advances by one original-index scope on unchanged chains if infinitude permits, and on chain change uses a greatest common infinite scope when one exists, otherwise scope one (`AlgorithmOneRun`:45,64,616; `RuleTwoReset`:31,38,53). This is an explicit semantic witness, not a literal transcription of the compressed-list Algorithm 1. Original-index gaps, initial universal intersection and finite transient differences do not weaken the claimed existence theorem. C⊆hypothesis occurs cofinally, not eventually; no index stabilization, density, bounded gap, or identification follows. Classification: faithful theorem realization with a changed witness presentation.

### 5. `R.theorem_2_2` — all three breadth/validity clauses

Overview:32; source p.10/PDF13. After the four standard assumptions, the actual nested conjunction is: (i) every `algorithmOneIndices` intersection is infinite; (ii) `∃T,∀t≥T, M_t⊆C z`; and (iii) `∀T,∃t≥T, E⊆M_t ∧ M_t⊆C z`. The simultaneous sandwich in (iii) matters; the target does not merely choose unrelated C-containing and valid stages. Its quantifier T is an arbitrary lower time bound, not one common stabilization threshold for all presentations.

The source first clause only promises infinitude on an eventual tail; Lean's all-stage infinitude is a faithful strengthening. Eventual validity plus cofinal full stages makes the added simultaneous target inclusion in clause (iii) equivalent to the source combination. The finite indices give actual conjunction representations. No eventual E-containment, hypothesis/index stabilization or element output is stated. These are generation/breadth hypotheses rather than identification. Native `theorem_2_2_freshOutput` supplies a separate element-generation consequence; no fresh element was smuggled into this target.

### 7. `R.theorem_3_1_eventual_validity` — warm-up validity only

Overview:36; most directly Lemma 3.2 p.17/PDF20, a component of Theorem 3.1. After an explicit infinitude proof and the remaining standard assumptions, it says **for every** `incomparable : Nat → Option AggressiveGuess`, there exists T after which `algorithmOneWarmupOutput ... incomparable t∈C z`. T may depend on that external choice. An aggressive guess packages an arbitrary infinite language and token; it has no built-in target accuracy, causality or fairness promise (`WarmupPriorityRun`:39,686,837,947).

Eventually comparable valid intersections make arbitrary guesses in the incomparable branch a finite-transient issue. This is a faithful generalization of the validity component, not the α/3 theorem. The run inserts input t into its used set before choosing a least element of the queue/current-language union, then records the output. Native `algorithmOneWarmupOutput_not_mem_sample_succ`:1025 and `..._injective`:973 provide current-input freshness and distinct outputs; those facts are omitted only from this facade conclusion. A concrete `sourceIncomparableChoice`:821/common-predecessor guess and `sourceWarmupOutput`:966 remove the unrestricted external parameter for a source-like semantic run. Full density does not follow merely by combining these validity statements.

### 8. `R.theorem_3_1_density_certificate` — conditional α/3 accounting

Overview:38; source accounting after Lemma 3.4, p.20–21/PDF23–24. The exact schema is `∀K enumerated output good bad (hcert : WarmupChargeCertificate ...), ∀α:Real, α≤d_K(enumerated) → α/3≤d_K(output)`. It neither selects an algorithm nor asserts existence of hcert. K, the four sets and all certificate data are fixed before α.

The constructor (`DensityAccounting`:84) requires `enumerated\output=good∪bad`, disjoint good/bad sets, finite exceptional bad ranks, and two total rank-charge functions. Good ranks and nonexceptional bad ranks charge to output members with rank≤i+1; each charge is separately injective on its relevant domain. The images may overlap, giving capacity two. There is no global injectivity condition on arbitrary unused ranks. A one-rank successor allowance and finite exceptions vanish in liminf accounting. No subset-of-carrier premises are required because density and charges restrict to carrier ranks. Empty good/bad/output sets are allowed when these assumptions hold; the carrier is necessarily infinite.

This is an exact/generalized version of the source's final conditional counting implication. Relative to Theorem 3.1's unconditional algorithm guarantee, its hypotheses are materially stronger and its conclusion weaker. Native source-run bad-charge fragments narrow the gap but remain conditional on coverage/return hypotheses. The missing complete certificate must not be inferred from its type name. Arbitrary real α and arbitrary ordered carriers extend the accounting lemma, not the generation theorem.

### 9. `R.theorem_3_5_fixed_pod_endgame` — conditional 1/2 passage

Overview:40; source final paragraph p.26/PDF29, not the whole p.21/PDF24 theorem. For fixed ordered K, fixed output set and fixed real α, if `∀s:Nat, 2≤s → α/(2+4/(s:Real))≤d_K(output)`, the conclusion is `α/2≤d_K(output)`. All arithmetic inside the bound is real; s is natural and 0,1 are excluded. No positivity premise is present. The target is an analytic implication; it contains no input stream, language family, input density, pod construction, validity or freshness assertion.

The final limit step faithfully matches the source. It cannot accommodate a different output set for every s, nor assert that an algorithm generated the fixed output. `FixedPodRatioCertificate` and `GrowingPodRatioCertificate` (`PodLimit`:116,156) package prefix inequalities with vanishing overhead for that same fixed output, and native theorems derive the corresponding bounds. These are useful conditional infrastructure. The complete native umbrella has no pod-generation state/run, no run-to-growing-certificate construction and no unconditional α/2 generation theorem. This is genuinely missing native mathematics, not just an omitted facade alias. Any source arithmetic typos discussed below do not justify silently replacing the missing run construction by the desired inequalities.

### 10. `R.theorem_4_9` — full-text identification iff countable T_D

Overview:42; source Theorem 1.9/4.9 p.7/33, especially PDF36–38. For every set X of languages whose members are nonempty, it states `IdentifiableOnFullTexts X ↔ X.Countable ∧ TDSpace X`. The left side is one arbitrary language-valued learner on finite ordered histories, working for every member and every full presentation, with eventual equality of returned sets to that language (`FullTextIdentification`:136; `AngluinToPaper02`:20,25,34). The source's eventually correct index language is represented extensionally here. A native indexed bridge also uses an actually stabilizing numerical name; no target-index knowledge is supplied to the learner.

Full opens are unions of finite-positive-data neighborhoods of subtype points in X (`FullTopology`:14,16,24). `Specializes K L` means every open containing K contains L, so its direction is underlying K⊆L. `TDPoint K` supplies an open O containing K such that every L∈O with L⊆K equals K (`:98,116`). This is precisely isolating K from its strict lower closure points, equivalent to closed `cl{K}\{K}`. It is not the stronger T_1 condition and not the τ_C quotient.

The characterization is faithful at the semantic level and generalized from infinite languages to finite nonempty languages. Nonemptiness avoids empty-language presentation vacuity; X itself may be empty. Countability is a conclusion as well as a sufficient condition and cannot be dropped because the string universe is countable. No computable learner, effective tell-tale enumeration, runtime or partial-data theorem is asserted. Native tell-tale equivalences supply Theorem 4.8's intended strict-subset condition independently of its printed formula ambiguity.

### 11. `R.corollary_4_10_full_text` — separation on full texts

Overview:44; source p.36/PDF39. Assuming all members of X nonempty, the type is `SeparatedInLimit X ↔ TOneSpace X`. The left expands over every ordered pair of distinct points K,L and every stream with range K to eventual persistent refutation of L by an observed string absent from L (`FullTextSeparation`:24,36,72,78). The right says `Specializes K L → K=L`. Both ordered directions are demanded across distinct languages; no uniform refuting string or threshold across presentations is claimed. There is no learner guessing a target in this separation predicate.

For the full-data interpretation of the corollary, this is faithful, and finite nonempty targets are a generalization. Relative to the source's preceding literal “possibly a subset” wording, it is a material restriction of the adversary. It must be called a full-text specialization, not silently treated as proving the partial assertion. Countability is unnecessary for this pairwise equivalence; the native separation-implies-identifiability theorem adds it. Empty and singleton classes make the pairwise conditions vacuous, while empty member languages remain excluded.

### 12. `R.corollary_4_10_partial_text_counterexample` — eleven concrete conjuncts

Overview:46; source comparison is with the partial-enumeration phrase preceding Corollary 4.10, p.36/PDF39. The closed target has no assumptions and specifies one fixed example: common stream n↦n+2, common set {2,3,…}, left language common∪{0}, right common∪{1}, and X={left,right} (`PartialSeparationCounterexample`:21–29).

Its eleven conjuncts are: X is T_1; common is infinite; left is infinite; right is infinite; left⊈right; right⊈left; the stream presents common; common⊆left; common⊆right; every finite prefix fails to refute left; and every finite prefix fails to refute right. Every component is substantive: the stream has infinite range and is a legitimate partial presentation for either target, the markers establish two incomparable distinct languages, and both universal nonrefutations hold even at empty history. Full-text T_1 does not separate these partial observations.

This is materially different from, and corrective evidence against, a literal partial-text version of the source corollary. It is not an affirmative formalization of that version. The selected conjunction itself does not quantify over all learners or negate a partial-separation predicate, although native `left_not_separated_from_right_on_infinite_partial_texts` and the converse do state that consequence. It does not refute partial **identification**: for this finite class the intersection common is a permissible successful hypothesis. Calling it an impossibility of conjunction identification would reverse the source/native meaning.

### 13. `R.corollary_4_11` — full separation iff inclusion antichain

Overview:48; source explicitly full-enumeration Corollary 4.11 p.36/PDF39. Assuming every X member nonempty, it equates the preceding full-text persistent-refutation property with `∀K L:Point X, underlying K⊆underlying L → K=L`. Equality is equality of subtype language points, not equality of arbitrary list indices. Because ordered pairs are universal, distinct languages are incomparable in both directions. This is faithful, generalized to finite nonempty languages and arbitrary class cardinalities.

No index learner, uniform observation bound or partial presentation is hidden in this equivalence. Empty/singleton classes are covered vacuously. A later observation of a witness in K\L makes refutation persist forever, but that guarantee uses full coverage of K; an infinite proper subset can omit all such witnesses. The theorem is compatible with target 12 and does not supply Theorem 4.12.

## Native coverage beyond the facade and remaining gaps

The native census positively supports more than the 13 wrappers. `FiniteScope.theorem_2_1`:151 and `SemiIndex` give the finite-scope generation implementation. `AlgorithmOneRun` has all-stage infinitude, eventual validity/comparability, cofinal fullness, finite-index representation, and `lemma_2_5_concrete_algorithmOne`:591. `RuleTwoReset` supplies observation-separated greatest-reset witnesses and leftmost-elimination data. Thus Lemma 2.5 and Algorithm 1 are not “missing mathematics” merely because they have no selected facade aliases. A printed algorithm's exact compressed indexing is different, but its semantic theorem obligations are genuinely represented.

`ElementSemiIndex.lemma_2_3_element_to_semiIndex`:215, `...semiIndex_to_element`:258 and `...generation_equivalence`:274 establish pointwise trace conversion for infinite presented subsets, and bound the produced number of indices by t. Their scopes contain an already supplied stream/output, and their types do not assert preservation of an effective deterministic learner or optimal upper/lower density. The generation-existence portion has native support; the density-optimality clause remains separate mathematics rather than a missing alias.

`WarmupPriorityRun` implements a priority run, the concrete common-predecessor choice and source-like warm-up output, eventual validity, distinct outputs, and freshness through the current input. `OrderedOccurrences` selects first occurrences after a cutoff, important when input streams repeat. Latest-return functions and injectivity theorems are present. In particular `sourceLatestReturnBadChargeFragment`:1691 constructs a bad-charge fragment only after assumptions that every relevant nonexceptional bad rank is `LateReturnEligibleRank` and that late outputs lie in the carrier. That eligibility includes occurrence, unseen-at-cutoff, absence from output, a prior return and a return strictly before the observation. These are mathematical conditions, not merely an already established run premise. Converting numerical inequalities to ordered rank inequalities additionally requires `InheritsAmbientOrder K`, whose exported value is strict monotonicity of K's enumeration.

`DensityAccounting` proves finite-prefix and liminf consequences from complete warm-up and capacity-one pod certificates; `PodLimit.fixedPod_finite_counting`:36 derives the coefficient 2+4/s from explicit natural-count inequalities. Fixed/growing ratio certificates and their bounds are also present. The complete census has no definition constructing a full `WarmupChargeCertificate` for the source warm-up run and no theorem discharging all its partition/good/bad obligations. The `sourceLatestReturnBadChargeFragment` is meaningful partial progress, not that certificate. Thus the gap for α/3 is incomplete native assembly plus unresolved run-level charging obligations, rather than absence of the entire accounting argument. ## Source issues and questions reserved for Stage 3

**The τ_C definition and Theorem 4.12 are incompatible as printed.** This is a source problem, independent of whether the theorem was formalized. On p.27/PDF30 and again p.39/PDF42, every L not containing all C has singleton {L} open. Every language containing C belongs to every non-singleton basic neighborhood U_F, so all such languages are topologically indistinguishable. Their Kolmogorov quotient collapses them to one point. For a non-containing L, choose c∈C\L: U_{ {c} } contains all full-C languages and excludes L. Conversely {L} excludes the full-C class. Distinct non-containing languages separate by their singleton opens. Therefore every two distinct quotient points separate in both directions: this quotient is always T_1 and hence T_D.

The condition in Theorem 4.12 would consequently hold for every class, including Example 2's {N}∪{N\{i}}, which the paper itself correctly presents as not fully, and therefore not partially, identifiable. The claimed specialization description on p.37/PDF40 cannot hold for a non-containing language with infinite C-trace, since its singleton open prevents specialization to any distinct point. This is stronger than identifying a failed proof step: the defined condition cannot furnish the asserted characterization. Replacing the singleton branch by a trace topology would change the source and has not been done here. Questions for Stage 3 are which exact source statement/topology is intended and how any corrected theorem would be labeled and proved.

**Separation wording genuinely changes the theorem.** The p.36/PDF39 definition permits enumerating a subset, whereas its full-positive-data proof requires eventually seeing a finite separating witness. Target 12 provides an explicit infinite common-subset obstruction even in a two-language T_1 class. Targets 11 and 13 faithfully state full-data results, not that literal partial claim. This does not impugn conjunction identification for finite families.

**Several construction/proof specifications need clarification without prejudging existential results.** Theorem 3.3's p.18/PDF21 recycling rule says to output the smallest learner-produced integer at every power of two; literally that can keep repeating the same integer and fail to enumerate other learner outputs. Its general-α argument assumes only a lower-density relation between K and K′, which alone does not justify the asserted upper-density product bound. These are failures of the stated witness/argument, not a proof that the matching existential sharpness theorem is false. The α/2 positive theorem likewise should not be judged false because of transcription errors in intermediate counting equations.

The pod algorithm p.22–23/PDF25–26 is written using the union of all past pods as a pending list without explicitly removing used strings in every emission line; a literal minimum of that permanent union would repeat its first element. The downward-movement branch does not clearly specify which language supplies its new strings.
