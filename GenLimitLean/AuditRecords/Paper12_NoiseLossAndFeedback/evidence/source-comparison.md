## Independent source inventory (recorded before Stage 1)

**Global conventions.** Section 2, p.3, defines a language as an infinite subset of a countably infinite universe; collections may be uncountable, and the default universe is the integers. Section 2.1, p.5, requires an injective complete presentation `x : N -> U`, with range exactly the target `K`. Round `t` exposes `x_0,...,x_t` before the generator acts. Correctness means membership in `K` and exclusion from that entire exposed prefix. Generated outputs may repeat. Definition 2.1 calls a generator a function on finite ordered input; no computable presentation, decidability, runtime, distribution, probability, or numerical loss is specified. The introduction's informal reference to input sets does not erase the ordered-history definition.

**Ordinary quantifiers.** Definitions 2.2-2.4, p.5, use one generator for the collection. Eventual generation has `forall K forall presentation exists T forall t >= T`; non-uniform has `forall K exists T forall presentation forall t >= T`; uniform has `exists T forall K forall presentation forall t >= T`. Definitions 2.5-2.7 introduce consistency, intersection closure, and closure dimension. Definitions 2.12-2.13, p.6, project by intersection with a subuniverse and discard finite projections. Definitions 5.5-5.6, p.14, define image collections and bijective isomorphism.

**Noisy models.** Definitions 2.8-2.11, pp.5-6, require an injective sequence covering every target element with a finite set of extraneous elements. Uniform and non-uniform thresholds are independent of the unknown noise level; eventual noisy thresholds may depend on the whole presentation. The paper explicitly excludes the intermediate noise-dependent uniform variants. Definitions 5.1-5.3, p.13, bound extraneous distinct elements by `i` (at most, not exactly), and permit a separate generator for each known `i`. No noisy model permits omissions.

**Lossy models.** Definitions 4.1-4.3, p.8, use one globally injective sequence with no observations. Uniform means one common tail contained in every language; non-uniform/eventual means a language-dependent tail. Definitions 4.8-4.10, p.10, allow any injective infinite stream contained in `K`, with no coverage requirement. “Infinite omissions” permits any number of omissions, including zero. Definitions 4.13-4.14, p.11, instead require range contained in `K` and at most `i` omitted target elements; the eventual threshold may depend on the stream.

**Feedback and identification.** Section 6.1 and Definitions 6.1-6.2, p.16, retain complete presentations and use the order example, adaptive membership query, truthful current answer, then output. Earlier answers are available before the current query; the current answer is available to the current output. Queries can be reconstructed, so need not be separate observed inputs. Infinite feedback permits one query each round. Definitions 6.5-6.6, p.18, permit abstention `bottom`, with bottom answer, and impose at most a fixed `i` genuine queries on every infinite input/answer sequence, not merely successful target runs. Definitions 6.8-6.9, pp.19-20, use explicitly indexed countable families and output indices. Correctness is equality of the denoted language, so duplicates in the indexing are permitted; non-uniform time depends on the target, independently of its presentation.

In the matrix, **E** means exact/equivalent on the paper's domain; **S** faithful specialization; **G** faithful generalization beyond that domain; **D** materially different literal object; **NR** not represented by that facade statement; **I** indeterminate pending clarification. ## Eight target dossiers

### Target 1.1 - existence versus the named construction

Source: pp.2 and 6-7. Facade: `P12/Results/Overview.lean:221`; explicit-type supplement confirms both hidden classes are over `Nat`. The three conjuncts have the right dependencies: two independently chosen injective autonomous sequences, a target-dependent threshold for the first, a common threshold for the second, and no ordinary eventual generator for their union. The source notes autonomous eventual and non-uniform generation coincide, so the first Lean predicate is equivalent to its wording. Autonomous positivity already forces every member infinite, despite no displayed `UUS` conjunct.

The source proof chooses all supersets of some nonnegative tail, and all supersets of the negative integers. The native named classes at `Overview:32,37` instead rename the pair from `Paper10_UnionClosednessOfLanguageGeneration/MinimalPairClasses.lean:25-46`. The autonomous non-uniform side consists of a finite initial negative segment plus the positive integers minus a finite set, with a further condition at index zero. The uniform side consists of all negative integers plus an arbitrary positive subset; neither contains integer zero before renaming. `Overview:43` explicitly packages countability of the first, uncountability of the second, both autonomous positives, and union impossibility. Thus this is a genuine named witness assembly, not merely an unnamed existential inferred from theorem names. `Overview:91` additionally packages ordinary non-uniform/uniform generation with the autonomous positives.

**Assessment:** the headline is faithfully represented; the concrete construction is materially different, and has an additional countability property on one side. These changes do not weaken the existential headline. The source's literal pair is not identified with the native pair, and no proof-body reconstruction is used to claim they coincide.

### Target 1.2 - four implications and unknown finite noise

Source: p.2, Definitions 2.8-2.11 pp.5-6, and Theorems 4.4-4.5 pp.8-9. Facade: `Overview:233`; native equivalences `NoisyWithoutSamples:424,434`. Both directions of both iff statements are present. The `Nat` universe is a faithful representation of the countably infinite source universe; the explicit `UUS C` merely exposes the source's global language convention.

Uniform noisy generation has `exists G exists T forall K forall noisy-stream forall t>=T`; non-uniform noisy generation has `exists G forall K exists T forall noisy-stream forall t>=T`. In particular neither threshold may depend on the finite noise bound or arrival pattern. Autonomous right sides choose a globally injective sequence, with the corresponding common or targetwise tail. This is not the weaker presentationwise noisy-limit condition, and is not a theorem about infinitely many contaminants. Neither side imposes effectiveness or a quantitative rate.

The defining types retain complete coverage of the target; the unknown contaminants are distinct because the entire presentation is injective. Prefix-dependent outputs are fresh relative to all observed values, including noisy ones. Existence-level equivalence does not identify the two witnesses or preserve a specified threshold. Native conversion definitions may use different sequence-selection details from Algorithms 1-2; their output-by-output identity is not a component of the theorem.

**Assessment:** exact/equivalent for infinite languages over the represented universe, with no opposing strength change. Empty collections remain valid on `Nat`; finite individual languages are excluded by the premise, consistently with the source.

### Target 1.3 - the increasing cover, including empty stages

Source: pp.2 and 9-10, with autonomous definitions p.8. Facade: `Overview:243`; native `NoSampleCharacterization:33,40,265`, using `Core/ClassCovers:14` and `Support/ClassIntersection:15`. The right side is one sequence of subclasses, monotone by inclusion, whose union equals `C` and whose common intersection is infinite at every stage. These are three separate obligations; no one is replaced by a mere covering inclusion, a pointwise fresh witness, or an infinite intersection of the whole collection.

The left side chooses one injective sequence before all targets; each target has its own eventual membership threshold. Every language appears at some stage and remains in later stages, matching the source witness dependencies. No effective enumeration of classes, algorithm to decide core membership, or uniform program extracting the sequence from the cover is asserted. The source's Algorithm 3 itself appeals to intersection membership without an effective representation assumption.

The Lean theorem assumes only `Countable α`, so it also treats finite or empty alphabets and classes with finite entries. On a finite alphabet no injective autonomous sequence exists and no stage can have an infinite intersection, even if `C` is empty. On an infinite alphabet the empty collection is allowed, with all stages empty and each empty intersection equal to the whole alphabet. Any finite language in a nonempty class makes both sides false. These boundaries do not change the source theorem on its stated domain.

**Assessment:** equivalent within scope and a faithful generalization outside it; neither direction has a hidden infinitude premise or a missing empty-stage obligation.

### Target 1.4 - the same generator survives arbitrary omissions

Source: p.3 and Theorems 4.11-4.12 p.11; relevant stream definitions p.10. Facade: `Overview:252`. Each conjunct universally quantifies the given generator and concludes correctness of that identical generator on arbitrary injective target-contained streams. This correctly retains the source's stronger same-algorithm formulation, rather than only preserving the class's generatability.

The first implication preserves a class-wide threshold order; the second preserves a targetwise threshold order. Neither statement asserts literal equality between chosen threshold witnesses. Both continue to judge freshness against `x_0,...,x_t` and allow repeated generated outputs. “Infinite omissions” does not require the omitted set actually be infinite: complete, finitely incomplete, and infinitely incomplete streams all qualify, but every observed stream still has infinite range. No invalid observation is permitted.

Native anchors `InfiniteOmissions:116,132` expose countability and infinitude explicitly. These are the source's ambient assumptions, not added restrictions on its intended language class. The general countable-alphabet form extends to finite alphabets only with an empty class under `UUS`; the implications are then vacuous. The result deliberately does not cover presentationwise eventual generation, whose stream-dependent threshold cannot justify the same prefix argument.

**Assessment:** equivalent on the paper's domain and a faithful vacuous extension to smaller alphabets. Both uniform and non-uniform variants, with their original information access and same witness, are represented.

### Target 1.5 - simultaneous noise and omission hierarchies

### Target 1.6 - separate known-bound generators versus one unknown-bound generator

### Target 1.7 - strictness, inclusions, and finite equivalence

Source: p.3; Definitions 6.1-6.6 and Theorems 6.3, 6.7 pp.16-19. Facade: `Overview:287` chooses one natural-universe class with mandatory-query eventual success, ordinary eventual impossibility, and impossibility for every fixed finite query budget. It directly represents strictness; it does not itself state the all-class equivalence between finite feedback and ordinary generation.

That universal component is native: `FiniteFeedback:578` states, for every class over a nonempty alphabet and every budget, `GeneratableInLimitWithQueries C budget iff GeneratableInLimitWithoutRepetitions C`. No strict difference in class-level generatability is inferred merely from the treatment of malformed histories; normalization would require a separate argument.

The source proof's isolated stronger uniform-feedback wording remains unresolved separately.

### Target 1.8 - semantic identification and least equal names

Source: pp.3 and 19-20. Facade: `Overview:297`, with `FeedbackIdentification:78,87`. The complete skeleton is `forall family C exists identifier I forall z exists T forall exact streams of C(z) forall t>=T, C(output(I,t))=C(z)`. The threshold is independent of the example presentation, and a truthful current membership response is available before the current index is output. No original target index is provided to the machine as observable data.

This matches Definition 6.9's semantic equality, including repeated names for the same language. The facade does not require the output index to stabilize or equal `z`. The stronger conclusion in the source's Algorithm 6 proof is nevertheless native: `algorithmSix_stabilizesTo_leastEqualName:246` states eventual equality of output indices to the least name for the target, with the same presentation-independent threshold. The definition `algorithmSix:133` queries `t` and chooses the least candidate at most `t`, consistent with both observed positives and membership answers (`:97,104,113`). This is not inferred merely from its name.

A natural-indexed family represents every nonempty countable class, including finite classes by repeated indices. `theorem_1_8_countable_class:327` explicitly supplies a class enumeration with exact range for countable nonempty classes; `Bridges/IndexedFamilyToClass:16` defines that representation. An empty collection has no target and its identification obligation is vacuous, but cannot be the range of a total natural-indexed family; the facade should not be described as literally quantifying empty class enumerations. Finite or empty entries are permitted by Lean but have no complete injective presentation, making their extra obligations vacuous.

**Assessment:** faithfully represented at the source's countable indexed, semantic level. Least-name stabilization is an additional native refinement, not a deficit; an effective learner for arbitrary uncomputably represented families remains outside both literal interfaces.

## Discrepancies, suspected source issues, and Stage 1 corrections

It changes the constructed collections, not the existential statement, and supplies a stronger cardinality refinement on the first class. The other apparent omissions are scope differences between the eight-target facade and the native library: 1.6's infinitude, 1.7's all-class equivalence, and 1.8's least-name stabilization all have explicit supporting statements.
