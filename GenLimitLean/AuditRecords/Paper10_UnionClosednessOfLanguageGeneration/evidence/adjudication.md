## Mathematical adjudication

**Infinite-language coupling and finite-member normalization.** Source notation and Remark 2.2, p. 7, require every target to be infinite. Let C,D denote the two signed deletion classes; F,S denote the full-negative and finite-negative-prefix classes of Theorem 4.3. All are sets of integer languages. Their defining expressions remain those reconstructed in Stage 2; zero is excluded and the omission sets have their stated signed domains.

For targets 1 and 7, normalization is valid under the actual assumptions. Restrict each existential family H to its infinite members. Finite subsets of countable Int form a countable set, so removing them preserves any asserted uncountability and preserves countability. Nonuniform and uniform generation pass to subclasses with the same generator and threshold dependencies. A finite language has no injective Nat-indexed exhaustive presentation; consequently injective-presentation generation imposes no condition on finite members. Removing them preserves union impossibility exactly. This recovers source-model existential families without claiming that target 2 identifies target 1's witnesses. Target 6 needs no normalization: an injective schedule eventually contained in each member already makes that member infinite.

Target 9 is different. EUC quantifies over possibly repetitive exhaustive streams, so a nonempty finite target has a valid presentation and finite cores. The weakness is concrete: take the uncountable class F of languages containing all negatives, and add the singleton target `{0}`. A generator always choosing a fresh negative succeeds on F even from distinct-sample threshold zero. For `{0}`, choose threshold two, which no sound stream reaches; the shared nonuniform requirement is vacuous. The augmented class is uncountable and nonuniform but fails EUC through `{0}`. Its infinite part F has an infinite common negative core and satisfies EUC. Therefore neither deletion of finite members nor a separate declaration `UUS C` repairs target 9's literal existential type.

Underlying coverage is nevertheless complete for the intended counterexample: `P/Theorem33UncountableEUC.lean:105` states `not Countable C and NonuniformlyGeneratable C and not EUC C` for that same named C. `P/Results/Detailed.lean:34` and target 10 state `UUS C`. These are directly composable claims about identical defining constants. The registry's canonical link solely to the weak existential is overbroad if read as an exact infinite-model specification; its contextual witness explanation is mathematically supported by those other types, not by the wrapper's name or comment.

**Specific witness, uncountability, and EUC.** Source Theorem 3.3 and its following sentence, p. 9, select the first 3.1 class. Detailed 4.4, p. 17, instead displays all finite deletions of negatives. Finite subsets of a countable set are countable, hence the latter class is countable and cannot alone discharge 3.3. The smallest repair is to retain 4.4 as its own valid theorem and supply the argument for the first 4.1 family, exactly the division of native statements.

Here is that argument, checking the actual omission constraints. The target of all negatives lies in C by deleting no negatives and every positive. For any finite negative sample S, every observed element is in its version-space core. Any unseen negative y can be excluded by the member `(Zminus minus {y})`, which belongs to C: its negative deletion is finite and its positive deletion is the infinite set of all positives. Positive points and zero are excluded by the all-negative target itself. Thus the core equals S at every prefix and EUC fails (`Theorem33UncountableEUC.lean:29,41,83`). C is uncountable: delete all positive even integers plus those positive odd integers indexed by an arbitrary subset of Nat; odd-point membership recovers that subset, while the even scaffold keeps the deletion infinite. This matches the scaffold/probe encoding and named uncountability interface at `Theorem41Cardinality.lean:194,236`. The symmetric argument gives D's EUC failure with all positives as target; no separate native second-side theorem was found. That is an optional missing endpoint, not a dubious source assertion.

**Autonomous schedules, ordinary freshness, and computation.** The source's p. 9 phrase “without requiring” adversary examples has no standalone definition; p. 15 exhibits the signed sweeps and p. 17 explicitly discusses the empty-input modification. The expressions `negativeCode n=-(n+1)` and `positiveCode n=n+1` (`P/SignedIntegers.lean:21,56`) are injective. The negative schedule belongs to every F member from time zero. For each S member, the positive schedule is in the target after its finite positive deletion set has been passed. These are the exact global/target-dependent quantifiers of the local no-input predicates. They are stronger concrete witnesses for the modified no-input reading, not a theorem that one history-blind sequence remains fresh against every contemporaneous adversary stream.

The freshening definition at `WithoutAdversaryInput.lean:85` selects `outputs (Nat.find ...)`, where the predicate is `n>=t` and the scheduled value is absent from the finite input sample; :68/:93 state existence and the precise specification. Injectivity ensures that a finite sample cannot exhaust the tail. If a schedule is correct after T, choose distinct-sample threshold d=T. Reaching d distinct observations implies elapsed time at least d, and every later output uses an index at least that later time, hence at least T. Membership and input freshness both follow. This verifies the bridge's dependencies, including target-dependent T for the nonuniform case.

Source and formal main claims allow arbitrary deterministic finite-history functions; neither supplies a membership oracle, stochastic input law, noise model, effective class enumeration, or runtime guarantee. The generic definition is `noncomputable` and accepts an arbitrary possibly noncomputable schedule. For the explicit integer schedules, the search is informally executable using integer equality and schedule evaluation, with no target-membership query, but comments and existence types do not certify a uniform program for arbitrary semantic schedules. Detailed 4.4 also admits the negative no-input schedule by the same finite-deletion argument, although its native wrapper asserts ordinary nonuniform generation, not a separate no-input predicate.

**Literal signs, uniformity, and phase repair.** The p. 11 display subtracts a positive A from negatives and a negative B from positives. Literally neither subtraction changes its half-line, so both families collapse to the nonzero integers and the claimed union difficulty disappears. Swapping the two domain restrictions is the smallest repair; it yields p. 13's detailed Theorem 4.1 and `P/MainClasses.lean:20,25,34` without changing which omission set is finite or infinite. This correction is accepted from the displayed formulas and prose, not merely from integration notes.

The final p. 17 uniformity sentence is false in both relevant readings. For any claimed ordinary uniform threshold d and generator, choose a history of d distinct negative values and inspect its output y. If y is already seen or nonnegative, it is wrong on the all-negative target. Otherwise the target `Zminus minus {y}` contains the history, is infinite, and makes that output wrong. The history extends to an injective exhaustive presentation, contradicting correctness at the threshold. This works for every d, including the empty-history boundary. For an autonomous common tail time T, delete a scheduled negative value at a time at least T; a nonnegative scheduled value already fails the all-negative target. Replacing “uniformly” by “non-uniformly” repairs the sentence; `P/EventuallyUnboundedClosure.lean:138,164` separately states the standard refutation and valid displayed theorem.

For 4.3's source proof, pp. 16-17, `w_t=-t-t1-1` starts below -1 and skips an initial block. That is compatible with cofinite negatives in 4.1 but not full negatives in F. Starting the first negative sweep at -1 and continuing without gaps repairs this coverage issue. The native initial state has `negativeCursor := 0` (`Internal/AlternatingEngine.lean:716`); its tail expression uses `negativeCode(d+k)` (:112), and explicit interfaces state every negative belongs to the limit (:1704) and the limit belongs to F (:1826). Both detailed lower bounds restrict a common hard subfamily defined by intersections (:43-64); hardness of that subfamily implies hardness of each containing union. This is a justified construction change at statement/invariant level, not proof equality. The source's claimed permanent-error transition remains imprecise; the native interface specifically preserves a positive omitted output forever (:1665), so the printed phase labels are not accepted as an identical proof script.

**Full-paper boundary.** Detailed 4.1/4.3/4.4, named cardinalities and EUC, and deterministic A.1 have the native endpoints recorded in Stage 2. Randomized A.2's one target/stream depending on the generator's law, almost-sure finitely many successes, and summability conclusion are not supplied by any deterministic endpoint. Remark 4.2's finite-target identification example likewise has no covering theorem. The abstract Appendix A.2 scheme correctly requires `SchemeLimitInAmbient` separately (`PrefixRealizability.lean:785,796`). Finite avoiding-prefix realizability alone does not ensure limit membership: finite prefixes containing only even naturals can extend to cofinite languages avoiding any finite set of odd naturals, yet their infinite even-only limit is not cofinite. The concrete row family remains absent; its printed `N x N` domain with row -1 and `A_i` under a k-indexed union require explicit source repairs before an instance is claimed. Existing notes disclose the coverage boundary but do not resolve those printed formulas.
